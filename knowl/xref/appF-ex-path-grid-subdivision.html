Checkpoint F.10.4 Optional: subdividing paths and parameter rectangles.

This exercise uses Checkpoint F.10.1 directly. Let \(P\) be a closed bounded interval or rectangle and \(H:P\to D\) continuous, where \(D\) is open. Show that some \(\delta>0\) ensures that every nonempty closed cell \(Q\subseteq P\) of diameter less than \(\delta\) has image in an open ball contained in \(D\text{.}\) If this fails, choose cells of diameter less than \(1/k\) and one point in each; extract a subsequence and use continuity at its limit. Explain why the same argument controls every point in each small cell.
🔗
Solution.
Choose failing cells \(Q_k\) and points \(u_k\in Q_k\text{.}\) A subsequence tends to \(u\in P\text{.}\) Since \(D\) is open, \(B(H(u),r)\subseteq D\) for some \(r>0\text{.}\) Continuity relative to \(P\) gives \(\eta>0\) such that \(\|H(v)-H(u)\|<r\) for \(v\in P\) within \(\eta\) of \(u\text{.}\) For every \(v\in Q_{k_j}\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \|v-u\|\le\operatorname{diam}(Q_{k_j})+\|u_{k_j}-u\| <1/k_j+\|u_{k_j}-u\|. \end{equation*}
Eventually this is less than \(\eta\text{,}\) independently of the chosen \(v\text{.}\) The entire image then lies in \(B(H(u),r)\text{,}\) contradicting failure. A grid with side lengths below \(\delta/\sqrt n\text{,}\) where \(n=1\) or \(2\text{,}\) has the required diameter bound. This proves the supplied path theorem in Theorem 13.5.9 and the rectangle fact used in Optional: local potentials and a continuous contraction.
