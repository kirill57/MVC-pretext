Checkpoint F.10.2 Optional: from continuity to one common distance bound.

This exercise uses Checkpoint F.10.1. A continuous map \(f:K\to\mathbb R^m\) on a closed bounded set is uniformly continuous if, for every \(\varepsilon>0\text{,}\) there is \(\delta>0\) such that \(\|f(x)-f(y)\|<\varepsilon\) whenever \(x,y\in K\) and \(\|x-y\|<\delta\text{.}\) Assume this fails. Explain why some fixed \(\varepsilon_0>0\) admits points \(x_k,y_k\in K\) with \(\|x_k-y_k\|<1/k\) but output distance at least \(\varepsilon_0\text{.}\) Extract a convergent subsequence of \(x_k\) and use continuity to reach a contradiction.
🔗
Solution.
Failure of the stated quantifiers gives such a fixed error and pairs for every allowed distance \(1/k\text{.}\) The preceding exercise gives \(x_{k_j}\to x\in K\text{.}\) The triangle inequality gives \(\|y_{k_j}-x\|\le1/k_j+\|x_{k_j}-x\|\to0\text{.}\) Continuity at \(x\) makes both output sequences tend to \(f(x)\text{,}\) so their difference tends to zero, contradicting the fixed lower bound. The empty-set case is immediate. This also proves the result for a continuous vector-valued map, since only distances were used.
