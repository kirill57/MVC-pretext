Aside: Optional: the completeness fact and vocabulary for this investigation.

We supply the nested-interval fact: decreasing nonempty closed bounded intervals whose lengths tend to zero have exactly one common point. Applying this in each coordinate gives the same result for nested closed boxes whose side lengths tend to zero. This fact expresses completeness of the real numbers; we do not prove completeness here.
🔗
A sequence is a list \(x_1,x_2,\ldots\text{.}\) A subsequence \(x_{n_1},x_{n_2},\ldots\) selects terms with strictly increasing indices. Convergence to \(x\) means that eventually all selected terms are within every prescribed positive distance of \(x\text{.}\) An open set contains a small open ball around each of its points; a set is closed when its complement is open. A bounded set lies in a finite box. The diameter of a box with side lengths at most \(\ell\) in \(\mathbb R^n\) is at most \(\sqrt n\,\ell\text{.}\)
