Here is the coordinate justification. Write \(\omega=\sum_i F_i\,dx_i\) and \(\mathbf r^*\omega=A\,du+B\,dv\text{,}\) where \(A=\sum_i F_i(\mathbf r)(x_i)_u\) and \(B=\sum_i F_i(\mathbf r)(x_i)_v\text{.}\) The product and chain rules give
\begin{equation*} B_u-A_v=\sum_{i,j}(\partial_jF_i)(\mathbf r) \bigl((x_j)_u(x_i)_v-(x_j)_v(x_i)_u\bigr). \end{equation*}
Terms containing \((x_i)_{uv}-(x_i)_{vu}\) cancel because \(\mathbf r\) is \(C^2\text{.}\) The displayed coefficient is exactly the coefficient obtained by pulling back \(d\omega=\sum_{i,j}\partial_jF_i\,dx_j\wedge dx_i\text{.}\) This proves the identity used here without assuming Stokes’ theorem.
