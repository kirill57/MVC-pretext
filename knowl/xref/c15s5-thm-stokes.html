Theorem 15.5.5 Stokes’ theorem.

Let \(S\) be a compact oriented piecewise smooth surface with piecewise smooth boundary
\begin{equation*} \partial S. \end{equation*}
Let
\begin{equation*} \mathbf F=(P,Q,R) \end{equation*}
have continuous first partial derivatives on an open region containing \(S\text{.}\) Give \(\partial S\) the orientation compatible with the chosen orientation of \(S\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} \boxed{ \oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = \iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}\,dS. } \end{equation*}
In differential-form notation, if
\begin{equation*} \omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz, \end{equation*}
then
\begin{equation*} \boxed{ \int_{\partial S}\omega = \int_S d\omega. } \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof for smooth patches; scope of the general statement.
First suppose one regular parametrization has continuous second partial derivatives and covers the surface once, with the usual boundary identifications. Parametrize this patch by
\begin{equation*} \mathbf r:D\to S, \end{equation*}
where \(D\) is a compact planar region with piecewise smooth boundary, and the parametrization is \(C^2\) on a neighborhood of \(D\text{.}\) Use the orientation given by
\begin{equation*} \mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v. \end{equation*}
The boundary of \(S\) is the image of the boundary of \(D\text{,}\) with the induced orientation.
🔗
Pull the \(1\)-form \(\omega\) back to the parameter domain:
\begin{equation*} \mathbf r^*\omega. \end{equation*}
This is now a \(1\)-form on a region in the \(uv\)-plane. By Green’s theorem,
\begin{equation*} \int_{\partial D}\mathbf r^*\omega = \int_D d(\mathbf r^*\omega). \end{equation*}
Here is the required chain-rule calculation. Write
\begin{equation*} \mathbf r^*\omega=A\,du+B\,dv,\qquad A=\mathbf F(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf r_u,\quad B=\mathbf F(\mathbf r)\cdot\mathbf r_v. \end{equation*}
The product and chain rules give
\begin{equation*} B_u-A_v=(D\mathbf F(\mathbf r)\mathbf r_u)\cdot\mathbf r_v -(D\mathbf F(\mathbf r)\mathbf r_v)\cdot\mathbf r_u. \end{equation*}
The terms \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r)\cdot(\mathbf r_{vu}-\mathbf r_{uv})\) cancel by equality of continuous mixed partials. Expanding the remaining dot products gives
\begin{equation*} B_u-A_v=(\nabla\times\mathbf F)(\mathbf r)\cdot (\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v). \end{equation*}
By the plane definition of \(d\) and the flux pullback formula of Section 15.4, this proves the particular identity
\begin{equation*} d(\mathbf r^*\omega)=\mathbf r^*(d\omega) \end{equation*}
used here, without assuming its later general version. Therefore
\begin{equation*} \int_{\partial D}\mathbf r^*\omega = \int_D \mathbf r^*(d\omega). \end{equation*}
Translating back to the surface gives
\begin{equation*} \int_{\partial S}\omega = \int_S d\omega. \end{equation*}
🔗
For a surface divided into finitely many such patches, add these identities. The line integrals on shared edges cancel because the induced directions are opposite. This proves the result for that class of surfaces. The more general piecewise smooth statement above is accepted here; extending this argument to arbitrary admissible patches requires an approximation argument not supplied in this book. Chapter 18 develops the unified theorem and its local mechanism.
