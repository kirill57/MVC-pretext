Checkpoint 19.5.12 Why averaging forces local constancy.

A continuous temperature on a circle is everywhere at most \(M\text{,}\) and its average is \(M\text{.}\) If its value is smaller than \(M\) somewhere, continuity makes it at most \(M-\varepsilon\) on a small arc for some \(\varepsilon>0\text{.}\) Use the positive length of that arc to show the average would be less than \(M\text{.}\) Now let a harmonic function attain its maximum \(M\) at an interior point. Apply the argument to every sufficiently small centered circle, using the mean value property, and show that it is constant on a whole disk. Does this local argument alone prove constancy throughout the entire region?
🔗
Solution.
Using angular coordinates, choose an arc of angle \(\ell>0\) on which the deficit is at least \(\varepsilon\text{.}\) The integral over the full circle is at most \(2\pi M-\varepsilon\ell\text{,}\) so the average is strictly less than \(M\text{,}\) a contradiction. Thus all values on that circle equal \(M\text{.}\) Choose a radius whose closed disk stays inside the harmonic domain. Every point of its open disk other than the center lies on one of the smaller centered circles. All these points, and the center itself, have value \(M\text{.}\) This proves local constancy; the supplied propagation fact is still needed for the conclusion on the whole connected region.
