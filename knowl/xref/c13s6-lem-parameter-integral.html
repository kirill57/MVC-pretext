Lemma 13.6.7 Differentiating an integral with a parameter.

Suppose \(q(x,t)\) and \(q_x(x,t)\) are continuous on a neighborhood of \([a,b]\times[c,d]\text{.}\) For \(a\lt x\lt b\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} H(x)=\int_c^d q(x,t)\,dt \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H'(x)=\int_c^d q_x(x,t)\,dt. \end{equation*}
The same conclusion holds for any fixed subinterval of \([c,d]\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Fix \(x\text{.}\) Continuity of \(q_x\) makes \(q_x(x+s,t)-q_x(x,t)\) uniformly small for all \(c\le t\le d\) when \(s\) is small. To justify the word “uniformly”, first use continuity near each \((x,t_0)\) to bound both values by their difference from \(q_x(x,t_0)\text{.}\) The closed interval \([c,d]\) is covered by finitely many of these neighborhoods, so the smallest of their allowed positive bounds on \(|s|\) works for the entire interval. This is the usual finite-cover argument behind uniform continuity on a closed bounded interval.
🔗
The one-variable Mean Value Theorem, applied with \(t\) fixed, now gives
\begin{equation*} \left|\frac{q(x+h,t)-q(x,t)}{h}-q_x(x,t)\right|\le\varepsilon \end{equation*}
for all \(t\in[c,d]\) and all sufficiently small nonzero \(h\text{.}\) Integrating this inequality yields
\begin{equation*} \left|\frac{H(x+h)-H(x)}{h}-\int_c^d q_x(x,t)\,dt\right| \le(d-c)\varepsilon. \end{equation*}
Letting \(h\to0\) proves the derivative formula. Thus the interchange is justified by the stated continuity hypotheses, not by a formal movement of symbols.
