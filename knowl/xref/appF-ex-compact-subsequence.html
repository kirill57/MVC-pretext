Checkpoint F.10.1 Optional: finding a convergent subsequence.

Use the supplied nested-box fact in Optional: the completeness fact and vocabulary for this investigation. Given a sequence in a nonempty closed bounded set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{,}\) enclose it in a closed box. Bisect every side and retain a subbox containing infinitely many terms (counted by their indices). Repeat. Choose one term in the \(k\)th box with index larger than every previously selected index. Show that these selected terms converge to the common point of the boxes. Why must that point belong to \(K\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution.
At each stage finitely many subboxes cover the previous box. If each held only finitely many indices, their union would too, a contradiction. Thus the construction continues, and infinitely many available indices permit the increasing selection. If the initial longest side is \(L\text{,}\) the \(k\)th box has diameter at most \(\sqrt n L/2^k\text{.}\) Completeness supplies a common point \(x\text{,}\) and the distance from the selected term to \(x\) is at most that diameter, hence tends to zero. If \(x\notin K\text{,}\) the open complement contains a ball about \(x\) disjoint from \(K\text{;}\) convergence would eventually put selected terms of \(K\) in that ball. Thus \(x\in K\text{.}\)
