Aside: Optional: oriented-volume connection and the spherical sign.

Use the pointwise triple-covector determinant from Optional: oriented-volume connection. Here \(dx,dy,dz\) are the differentials of the three coordinate functions on the same \((\rho,\phi,\theta)\) parameter domain. The signed meridional determinant is
\begin{equation*} \frac{\partial(r,z)}{\partial(\rho,\phi)} =\det\begin{pmatrix}\sin\phi&\rho\cos\phi\\ \cos\phi&-\rho\sin\phi\end{pmatrix}=-\rho. \end{equation*}
Appending \(\theta\) gives order \((r,z,\theta)\text{.}\) Swapping to \((r,\theta,z)\) changes the sign, giving \(+\rho\text{.}\) The cylindrical Cartesian determinant in that order is \(r\text{,}\) so composition gives
\begin{equation*} \frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(\rho,\phi,\theta)} =\rho r=\rho^2\sin\phi,\qquad dx\wedge dy\wedge dz =\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\wedge d\phi\wedge d\theta. \end{equation*}
Exchanging the two angular variables reverses the signed determinant. Ordinary volume uses its absolute value in either orientation. The order of a scalar iterated integral is a separate choice.
