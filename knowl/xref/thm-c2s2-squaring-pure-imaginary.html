Theorem 2.2.12 Squaring a pure imaginary quaternion.

Let
\begin{equation*} V=xi+yj+zk. \end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*} V^2=-(x^2+y^2+z^2). \end{equation*}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation*} V^2=-\|V\|^2. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Expand:
\begin{align*} V^2 \amp=(xi+yj+zk)(xi+yj+zk). \end{align*}
The square terms give
\begin{equation*} x^2i^2+y^2j^2+z^2k^2 = -x^2-y^2-z^2. \end{equation*}
The mixed terms come in opposite pairs:
\begin{equation*} xy(ij+ji)=xy(k-k)=0, \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} xz(ik+ki)=xz(-j+j)=0, \end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*} yz(jk+kj)=yz(i-i)=0. \end{equation*}
Thus
\begin{equation*} V^2=-(x^2+y^2+z^2). \end{equation*}
