# Exact diagnostic probes

These calculations test specific proposed inferences. They are mathematical reconstructions, not numerical proof claims. Source-facing dispositions are in the findings/proof-status files.

1. **Continuous image need not be a surface.** `(s,t)->(s,0,0)` has a line image; a constant map has a point image. Mere continuity/two-input notation supplies no regular surface theorem. The geometric introduction allows degenerate maps; the later regularity condition is substantive.
2. **Map defect versus image defect.** `(s,t)->(s^3,t,0)` covers a plane; velocities `(3s^2,0,0),(0,1,0)` become dependent when `s=0`, but `(a,b)->(a,b,0)` is regular. For the cone `(s cos t,s sin t,s)`, the velocities are `(cos t,sin t,1),(-s sin t,s cos t,0)` and cross product `s(-cos t,-sin t,1)`. At the vertex the first generator directions for `t=0,pi/2,pi` have determinant2 and cannot all lie in one plane. A genuine tangent-plane failure is established, not inferred only from a zero cross product.
3. **Sphere pole is a chart defect.** `r(phi,theta)=(sin phi cos theta,sin phi sin theta,cos phi)` has `r_theta=0` at the poles. Near the north pole, `(u,v,sqrt(1-u^2-v^2))` is a regular graph. Keep this separate from the cone.
4. **Partials do not imply differentiability.** Set `f(x,y)=xy/(x^2+y^2)` off0 and `f(0)=0`. Coordinate partials at0 are both0, while along `x=y!=0` the value is1/2, so it is not even continuous. A derivative matrix made of existing partials is not automatically a total derivative.
5. **Unequal mixed partials and the domain of d.** Let `g=xy(x^2-y^2)/(x^2+y^2)` off0, `g(0)=0`. Homogeneity and bounded rational angular factors give `g=O(r^2)` and first derivatives `O(r)`, so g is C1. Axis computations give `g_x(0,y)=-y`, `g_y(x,0)=x`; hence `(g_x)_y(0)=-1`, `(g_y)_x(0)=1`. These first-derivative coefficients are not C1 near0. Therefore the scalar curl expression has value2, but `d(dg)` under the book's C1-form operator domain is undefined there. This supports the repair to17.4, not a false theorem claiming d-squared is nonzero on its stated domain.
6. **Hole obstructs global potential.** For `alpha=(-y dx+x dy)/(x^2+y^2)`, direct differentiation gives `P_y=Q_x`. Pull back along `(R cos t,R sin t)` to get `dt`; integral from0 to2pi is2pi. A global single-valued potential would force0 by the already proved line-integral FTC.
7. **Orientation is not scalar integration order.** For `T(u,v)=(u,1-v)`, determinant is-1. Scalar area of the unit square is1 using absolute determinant; oriented pullback of `dx wedge dy` is `-du wedge dv`. Reordering scalar integration bounds via Fubini does not change the ordinary area to-1.
8. **Double coverage.** Polar coordinates with `0<=r<=1,0<=theta<=4pi` produce integral `integral r dr dtheta=2pi`, twice the disk area. Complex squaring `(u,v)->(u^2-v^2,2uv)` has determinant `4(u^2+v^2)` and also gives2pi over the unit disk. Boundary coordinate degeneracy does not explain or excuse this repeated interior coverage.
9. **Nonlinear constant Jacobian.** `(u,v)->(u,v+u^2)` is nonlinear, globally invertible by `(x,y)->(x,y-x^2)`, and has determinant1 everywhere. Thus nonlinearity does not require a varying determinant. The current Chapter12 checkpoint handles this correctly.
10. **Pointwise limit versus integral.** For `f_n(t)=n^2 t(1-t)^n`, both endpoints have value0. For fixed `0<t<1`, exponential decay dominates `n^2`, so `f_n(t)->0`. Substitute `u=1-t` to obtain `integral f_n=n^2(1/(n+1)-1/(n+2))=n^2/((n+1)(n+2))->1`. This refutes a pointwise-only limit exchange, not the stronger parameter-integral lemma's conclusion under its actual hypotheses.
11. **Missing regularity in Chapter9 assessments.** `F=z^2` and `g=(x^2+y^2-1)^2` give smooth level images with zero defining gradient; `f=x` has a constrained circle extremum with nonzero gradient. A smooth image alone cannot replace a nonzero defining gradient.
12. **Isolated-point continuity.** A function on the singleton domain `{a}` is continuous at a under the relative epsilon-delta definition. The book's punctured-limit convention requires an accumulation point, so the unqualified three-condition review criterion is not necessary there.

The sector sign calculation used in the proposed Chapter11 repair is `det d(r,z)/d(rho,phi)=-rho` for `r=rho sin phi,z=rho cos phi`. Its unsigned area factor is rho. Multiplying the cylindrical factor r gives `rho^2 sin phi`; the ordered Cartesian spherical triple `(rho,phi,theta)` has positive determinant. The repair must keep these two orientation conventions explicit.
