**Plane versus cone probe, exact computation.** For the plane map q(s,t)=(s³,t,0), the frozen velocities are (3s²,0,0),(0,1,0); their cross product is zero when s=0. Yet q covers the ordinary xy-plane, which has the regular parametrization (a,b,0) with cross product (0,0,1). Thus zero chart cross product alone does not prove a geometric singularity. For the double cone r(s,t)=(s cos t,s sin t,s), the cross product is (-s cos t,-s sin t,s), nonzero precisely when s≠0. At the vertex, the three generator velocities (1,0,1),(0,1,1),(-1,0,1) have determinant 2. Any tangent plane containing the first-order velocities of all three straight generator curves would need to contain three independent directions, which is impossible. The book makes this stronger argument explicitly at lines 690–696, then gives the plane counterexample at `c5s2-regularity-map-versus-image`, lines 744–751. The source figure at lines 698–742 correctly labels ordinary v,w,N and distinguishes the vertex.

**Sphere-pole probe.** The spherical parametrization r(theta,phi)=(a sin phi cos theta,a sin phi sin theta,a cos phi) collapses the theta edge at phi=0, so its theta velocity vanishes there. The sphere itself is regular at the north pole: the upper graph map q(u,v)=(u,v,sqrt(a²-u²-v²)) has, at (0,0), frozen velocities (1,0,0),(0,1,0) and normal (0,0,1). The graph domain includes a disk neighborhood of (0,0). The tangent plane is z=a. Section4.6 explicitly supplies the graph parametrizations at lines 529–549, so this probe uses book-given maps and only the later ordinary derivative capability from5.2. The degeneration belongs to the spherical coordinates, not to the sphere. No derivative language is needed in the earlier4.5 seam task.

**Partial-derivative insufficiency probe.** Set g(x,y)=x²y/(x²+y²) off the origin and g(0,0)=0. Both coordinate partials are zero. The estimate abs(g)<=abs(y)<=sqrt(x²+y²) proves continuity. Differentiability would force L=0, but on (t,t) the quotient g(t,t)/sqrt(2t²)=sign(t)/(2sqrt2) does not tend to zero. This is the exact obstruction used in7.6 and7.7. The still stronger example f=x³/(x²+y²), f(0)=0, has directional derivatives a³ for every unit direction (a,b), which are not linear in the direction; partials force L(x,y)=x and the diagonal contradicts it. Conversely f=x² sin(1/x), extended0 and independent of y, is differentiable with L=0 because abs(f)/sqrt(x²+y²)<=abs(x), although fx is discontinuous. These three probes separately distinguish partial existence, all-directional existence, differentiability and continuous partials.
