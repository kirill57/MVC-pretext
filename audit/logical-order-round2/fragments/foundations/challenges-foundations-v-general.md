## Protected judgments challenged

1. **Continuous homotopy, moving base point, and polygon cancellation — sustained.** At `c17s5-thm-simply-connected-potential`, lines 449–495, H need only be continuous and H(0,s)=H(1,s); the common endpoint can move. I checked the apparent shared-edge problem: each cell may have a different convex ball, but the straight segment between the same two image vertices is literally the same segment for both neighboring cells. Because both endpoints lie in each cell's ball, that segment lies in both balls. Interior edges therefore cancel exactly, side polygons cancel by equality of the endpoint paths, and original bottom arcs can be replaced using local potentials. No differentiability of H and no embedded spanning surface are needed. GF-01 is the separate uniform-subdivision premise.

2. **Supplied Stokes and noncircular surface-chart proof — sustained.** `source/chapters/ch18-generalized-stokes/sections/sec-18-the-theorem.xml:71–94` expressly supplies the k=1,2,3 theorem and labels the general patching estimates an outline. It is an honest proof-graph root. In `sec-18-classical-stokes-theorem-as-generalized-stokes.xml:197–209`, write A=sum Fi(r) xi,u and B=sum Fi(r) xi,v. Product differentiation gives B_u-A_v=sum partial_j Fi(r)(xj,u xi,v-xj,v xi,u), with the C2 mixed-coordinate terms cancelling. This is the pullback coefficient of d omega independently of Stokes. Applying planar Green afterwards is not circular.

3. **Cone truncation — sustained.** `c18s10-ex-cap3-cone`, lines 808–827, explicitly excludes the apex before applying the smooth theorem. For r=(s cos(theta),s sin(theta),1-s), pullback of 2 dx wedge dy is 2s ds wedge dtheta. The annular integral is 2pi(1-epsilon²); the clockwise inner circle contributes -2pi epsilon² and the omitted tip contributes +2pi epsilon². Both corrections tend to zero. This is a valid limiting argument, unlike applying smooth-surface Stokes directly at a cone vertex.
