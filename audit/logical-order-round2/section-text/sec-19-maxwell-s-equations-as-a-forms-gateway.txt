[5-5 title sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
Maxwell's equations as a forms gateway

[7-7 p sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
This section is the main capstone of the book.

[9-13 p sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
It is not an electromagnetism course. We will not derive every physical constant or solve
    realistic engineering problems. The goal is different: to see why the language of forms is
    not decoration.

[15-17 p sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
Maxwell's equations are the place where the main ideas of the book meet:

[20-20 li sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
fields attached to space,

[21-21 li sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
circulation along curves,

[22-22 li sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
flux through surfaces,

[23-23 li sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
sources inside volumes,

[24-24 li sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
the exterior derivative d,

[25-25 li sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
the identity d^2=0,

[26-26 li sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
and conservation laws.

[29-33 p sec-19-maxwell-s-equations-as-a-forms-gateway preview_or_optional_candidate]
Standard vector calculus treats these as four distinct formulas. The language of forms strips
    that away, unifying them into simple rules about boundaries and derivatives. The math doesn't
    obscure the physics; it reveals the underlying structure.

[36-36 title c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
Electric and magnetic fields

[38-44 p c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
In the smooth calculations below, assume the field coefficients have continuous
      second derivatives in space and time on the regions used. The integration surfaces
      and solids are fixed in time. Time derivatives act on the coefficients of a spatial
      form, leaving dx,dy,dz fixed. The integral-differentiation argument in
       then applies in fixed parameter domains.

[46-46 p c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
Electric and magnetic fields do different kinds of measuring.

[48-54 p c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
An electric field measures work per unit charge along a small displacement. That makes it
      naturally a 1-form:
      \boxed{\mathcal E=E_1\,dx+E_2\,dy+E_3\,dz.}
      If a unit positive charge moves by a tiny vector, \mathcal E measures the electric
      work along that tiny motion.

[56-61 p c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
A magnetic field is naturally measured by flux through an oriented surface. So we write the
      magnetic field as a 2-form:
      \boxed{\mathcal B=B_1\,dy\wedge dz+B_2\,dz\wedge dx+B_3\,dx\wedge dy.}
      This form measures magnetic flux through small oriented area pieces.

[63-69 p c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
Two related fields are also useful. The electric displacement field is a flux
      2-form:
      \boxed{\mathcal D=D_1\,dy\wedge dz+D_2\,dz\wedge dx+D_3\,dx\wedge dy.}
      The magnetic intensity field is a circulation 1-form:
      \boxed{\mathcal H=H_1\,dx+H_2\,dy+H_3\,dz.}

[71-76 p c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
In vacuum, the pairs are related by physical constants:
      \mathbf D=\varepsilon_0\mathbf E,\qquad \mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf H.
      In materials, the relations can be more complicated. Those relations belong to physics. The
      forms structure of Maxwell's equations does not depend on their simplest vacuum form.

[78-83 p c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
Charge density is stored in volume, so it is a 3-form:
      \boxed{\mathcal Q=\rho_{\mathrm e}\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.}
      Current density is flux of charge through surfaces, so it is a 2-form:
      \boxed{\mathcal J=J_1\,dy\wedge dz+J_2\,dz\wedge dx+J_3\,dx\wedge dy.}

[85-85 p c19s4-subsec-electric-and-magnetic-fields preview_or_optional_candidate]
The roles are summarized below.

[109-109 title c19s4-subsec-four-equations-in-forms-language preview_or_optional_candidate]
The four equations in forms language

[111-118 p c19s4-subsec-four-equations-in-forms-language preview_or_optional_candidate]
At each fixed time, Maxwell's equations can be written as
      \boxed{d\mathcal D=\mathcal Q,}
      \boxed{d\mathcal B=0,}
      \boxed{d\mathcal E+\frac{\partial\mathcal B}{\partial t}=0,}
      and
      \boxed{d\mathcal H-\frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial t}=\mathcal J.}

[120-122 p c19s4-subsec-four-equations-in-forms-language preview_or_optional_candidate]
These are Maxwell's equations in three-dimensional space, written with spatial forms.

[161-161 title c19s4-subsec-gauss-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
Gauss's law: charge is detected by electric flux

[163-168 p c19s4-subsec-gauss-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
The equation
      d\mathcal D=\mathcal Q
      says that the exterior derivative of the electric flux form is the charge density
      3-form.

[170-177 p c19s4-subsec-gauss-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
Let E be a solid region. Stokes' theorem gives
      \int_{\partial E}\mathcal D=\int_E d\mathcal D=\int_E\mathcal Q.
      So
      \boxed{\int_{\partial E}\mathcal D=\int_E \rho_{\mathrm e}\,dV.}
      In words:
      \boxed{\text{electric flux through a closed surface}=\text{charge enclosed.}}

[179-179 p c19s4-subsec-gauss-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
The boundary surface detects what is stored inside.

[182-182 title c19s4-ex-point-charge-from-outside preview_or_optional_candidate]
A point charge, seen from outside

[184-189 p c19s4-ex-point-charge-from-outside preview_or_optional_candidate]
A positive point charge at the origin creates electric flux through every sphere around
          it. In a fully realistic treatment, the charge density is concentrated at a point, so it
          is not an ordinary continuous function. The smooth version of Stokes' theorem in this
          book does not directly handle that singularity.

[190-195 p c19s4-ex-point-charge-from-outside preview_or_optional_candidate]
But the moral is exactly the same as the warning examples in
          : a closed surface can detect something inside. If a
          field is singular at a missing point, we cannot apply the ordinary smooth theorem across
          that point as though nothing were there.

[199-202 p c19s4-subsec-gauss-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
For smooth charge density, there is no singularity. The law simply says that charge is the
      source of electric flux.

[206-206 title c19s4-subsec-no-magnetic-monopoles preview_or_optional_candidate]
No magnetic monopoles: magnetic flux has no source

[208-212 p c19s4-subsec-no-magnetic-monopoles preview_or_optional_candidate]
The magnetic equation
      d\mathcal B=0
      says that magnetic flux has no volume source.

[214-221 p c19s4-subsec-no-magnetic-monopoles preview_or_optional_candidate]
Let E be a solid region. Then
      \int_{\partial E}\mathcal B=\int_E d\mathcal B=0.
      Thus
      \boxed{\int_{\partial E}\mathcal B=0.}
      In vector language,
      \boxed{\iint_{\partial E}\mathbf B\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}\,dS=0.}

[223-226 p c19s4-subsec-no-magnetic-monopoles preview_or_optional_candidate]
Magnetic field lines may pass through a closed surface, but as much flux enters as leaves.
      There is no isolated magnetic charge inside the region.

[229-229 title c19s4-ex-uniform-field-through-box preview_or_optional_candidate]
A uniform magnetic field through a box

[231-240 p c19s4-ex-uniform-field-through-box preview_or_optional_candidate]
Let
          \mathbf B=(0,0,B_0),
          so
          \mathcal B=B_0\,dx\wedge dy.
          For a rectangular box, the top face has upward orientation and contributes positive flux
          B_0(\text{area of top}).
          The bottom face has downward orientation and contributes the negative of the same amount.
          The side faces contribute 0, because the field is tangent to them.

[241-244 p c19s4-ex-uniform-field-through-box preview_or_optional_candidate]
The total magnetic flux through the closed box is
          0.

[248-251 p c19s4-subsec-no-magnetic-monopoles preview_or_optional_candidate]
Again the sign is not decoration. Flux through a closed surface is an oriented sum. Entering
      and leaving must be counted with opposite signs.

[255-255 title c19s4-subsec-faradays-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
Faraday's law: changing magnetic flux creates electric circulation

[257-269 p c19s4-subsec-faradays-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
Faraday's law is
      \boxed{d\mathcal E+\frac{\partial\mathcal B}{\partial t}=0.}
      Let S be an oriented surface with boundary \partial S. Integrate over
      S:
      \int_S d\mathcal E+\int_S \frac{\partial\mathcal B}{\partial t}=0.
      By Stokes' theorem,
      \int_S d\mathcal E=\int_{\partial S}\mathcal E.
      So
      \boxed{\int_{\partial S}\mathcal E=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_S\mathcal B.}
      In words:
      \boxed{\text{electric circulation around a loop}=-\text{rate of change of magnetic flux through the loop.}}

[271-275 p c19s4-subsec-faradays-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
This is one of the cleanest places to see why a 1-form and a 2-form belong in
      the same theorem. The electric field is integrated around a boundary curve. The magnetic
      field is integrated through a surface spanning that curve.

[278-278 title c19s4-ex-changing-field-circular-loop preview_or_optional_candidate]
A changing magnetic field through a circular loop

[280-284 p c19s4-ex-changing-field-circular-loop preview_or_optional_candidate]
Let S be the disk
          x^2+y^2\le R^2,\qquad z=0,
          oriented upward. Its boundary circle is oriented counterclockwise as viewed from above.

[285-292 p c19s4-ex-changing-field-circular-loop preview_or_optional_candidate]
Suppose the magnetic field is vertical and spatially uniform:
          \mathcal B=b(t)\,dx\wedge dy.
          Then
          \int_S\mathcal B=b(t)\pi R^2.
          Faraday's law gives
          \boxed{\int_{\partial S}\mathcal E=-b'(t)\pi R^2.}

[293-297 p c19s4-ex-changing-field-circular-loop preview_or_optional_candidate]
If b'(t)\gt 0, then the upward magnetic flux is increasing, and the induced
          electric circulation is negative relative to the counterclockwise orientation. That means
          the electric circulation is clockwise when viewed from above.

[298-301 p c19s4-ex-changing-field-circular-loop preview_or_optional_candidate]
The minus sign is the mathematical form of Lenz's law: the induced circulation opposes
          the change in magnetic flux.

[305-317 p c19s4-subsec-faradays-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
We can even find one possible electric form in this symmetric example. Recall that
      d(-y\,dx+x\,dy)=2\,dx\wedge dy.
      If
      \mathcal E=-\frac{b'(t)}{2}(-y\,dx+x\,dy),
      then
      d\mathcal E=-b'(t)\,dx\wedge dy.
      Since
      \mathcal B_t=b'(t)\,dx\wedge dy,
      we get
      d\mathcal E+\mathcal B_t=0.
      So this \mathcal E satisfies Faraday's law.

[321-321 title c19s4-subsec-ampere-maxwell-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
Ampere--Maxwell law: current and changing electric flux create magnetic circulation

[323-328 p c19s4-subsec-ampere-maxwell-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
The fourth equation is
      \boxed{d\mathcal H-\frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial t}=\mathcal J.}
      Equivalently,
      \boxed{d\mathcal H=\mathcal J+\frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial t}.}

[330-339 p c19s4-subsec-ampere-maxwell-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
Integrate over an oriented surface S:
      \int_S d\mathcal H=\int_S\mathcal J+\frac{d}{dt}\int_S\mathcal D.
      By Stokes' theorem,
      \int_S d\mathcal H=\int_{\partial S}\mathcal H.
      Therefore
      \boxed{\int_{\partial S}\mathcal H=\int_S\mathcal J+\frac{d}{dt}\int_S\mathcal D.}
      In words:
      \boxed{\text{magnetic circulation around a loop}=\text{current through the loop}+\text{changing electric flux through the loop.}}

[341-346 p c19s4-subsec-ampere-maxwell-law preview_or_optional_candidate]
The second term,
      \frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial t},
      is Maxwell's displacement-current term. It is not a decorative correction. It is the term
      that makes the equation compatible with conservation of charge.

[350-350 title c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
One loop, two surfaces: why Maxwell's correction is necessary

[352-352 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
This example is the heart of the capstone.

[354-358 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
Picture a wire charging a parallel-plate capacitor. A current flows through the wire toward
      one plate. Between the plates there is no conduction current crossing the gap, but the
      electric field between the plates is changing.

[360-362 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
Let C be a loop around the wire. The loop can be spanned by many different surfaces.

[364-368 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
One surface, call it S_1, cuts through the wire. The current flux through S_1
      is
      \int_{S_1}\mathcal J=I.

[370-374 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
Another surface, call it S_2, bulges out between the capacitor plates and crosses the
      gap instead of cutting the wire. No conduction current crosses this surface:
      \int_{S_2}\mathcal J=0.

[376-384 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
If the law were only
      d\mathcal H=\mathcal J,
      then Stokes' theorem would give
      \int_C\mathcal H=\int_{S_1}\mathcal J=I
      using S_1, but
      \int_C\mathcal H=\int_{S_2}\mathcal J=0
      using S_2.

[386-389 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
That is impossible. The same boundary loop C cannot have two different magnetic
      circulations.

[392-392 title checkpoint-19-4-surface-independence preview_or_optional_candidate]
Why should the surface not matter?

[394-400 p checkpoint-19-4-surface-independence preview_or_optional_candidate]
The contradiction came from getting two different answers for \int_C\mathcal H
          depending on whether we spanned the loop C with S_1 or S_2. Why
          should a quantity attached to the boundary loop be blind to the choice of
          spanning surface? What does this expectation reveal about the kind of object
          \mathcal J+\mathcal D_t has to be?

[403-408 p checkpoint-19-4-surface-independence hint]
Think about Stokes' theorem: \int_{\partial S}\mathcal H=\int_S d\mathcal H.
          If the left side depends only on the loop, what must be true of the right side as we
          slide the surface around? Closing a small gap between S_1 and S_2 turns
          two surfaces into the boundary of a solid region.

[412-416 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
Maxwell's corrected law says
      d\mathcal H=\mathcal J+\mathcal D_t.
      Now the two surfaces agree.

[418-425 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
For S_1, the conduction current contributes
      \int_{S_1}\mathcal J=I.
      For S_2, the changing electric flux contributes
      \frac{d}{dt}\int_{S_2}\mathcal D=I.
      So both surfaces give
      \boxed{\int_C\mathcal H=I.}

The same loop can bound different surfaces. Maxwell's displacement-current term makes the
        answer independent of the spanning surface.

[484-489 p c19s4-subsec-one-loop-two-surfaces preview_or_optional_candidate]
This example shows the power of the forms viewpoint. A boundary integral should not depend

[493-493 title c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
Charge conservation from d^2=0

[495-495 p c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
Now the continuity equation returns.

[497-515 p c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
Start with Ampere--Maxwell:
      d\mathcal H-\mathcal D_t=\mathcal J.
      Apply d to both sides:
      d(d\mathcal H)-d(\mathcal D_t)=d\mathcal J.
      Since
      d^2=0,
      we have
      d(d\mathcal H)=0.
      Also, equality of the continuous mixed space-time partial derivatives gives
      commutation of time differentiation with the spatial exterior derivative:
      d(\mathcal D_t)=(d\mathcal D)_t.
      By Gauss's law,
      d\mathcal D=\mathcal Q.
      Therefore
      -\mathcal Q_t=d\mathcal J.
      So
      \boxed{\mathcal Q_t+d\mathcal J=0.}

[517-517 p c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
This is charge conservation.

[520-520 title checkpoint-19-4-conservation-built-in preview_or_optional_candidate]
Built in, not bolted on?

[522-527 p checkpoint-19-4-conservation-built-in preview_or_optional_candidate]
We derived charge conservation after writing down Maxwell's equations, yet the
          text insists conservation was there all along. In what sense is conservation a
          consequence of the structure rather than an extra physical law we chose to add?
          What role does d^2=0 play in making it unavoidable?

[530-534 p checkpoint-19-4-conservation-built-in hint]
Notice that the only physics we used was Ampere--Maxwell and Gauss. The step
          d(d\mathcal H)=0 is pure geometry, true for any form. Ask yourself what
          would have to fail for charge not to be conserved.

[538-545 p c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
In vector notation,
      \mathcal Q=\rho_{\mathrm e}\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz,
      and
      d\mathcal J=(\nabla\cdot\mathbf J)\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.
      Thus
      \boxed{\rho_{\mathrm e,t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf J=0.}

[547-550 p c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
This is the continuity equation from , now forced
      by Maxwell's equations.

[552-552 p c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
The logic is worth pausing over:

[554-560 md c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
      &\text{Ampere--Maxwell law}\\
      +\ &\text{Gauss's law}\\
      +\ &d^2=0
      \end{aligned}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{conservation of charge.}}

[562-565 p c19s4-subsec-charge-conservation-d-squared preview_or_optional_candidate]
This is one of the deepest payoffs of the notation. Conservation is not pasted onto the
      equations afterward. It is built into their differential-form structure.

[569-569 title c19s4-subsec-homogeneous-equations-and-potentials preview_or_optional_candidate]
The homogeneous equations and potentials

[571-577 p c19s4-subsec-homogeneous-equations-and-potentials preview_or_optional_candidate]
Two of Maxwell's equations have no source terms:
      d\mathcal B=0,
      and
      d\mathcal E+\mathcal B_t=0.
      They are called the homogeneous equations.

[579-586 p c19s4-subsec-homogeneous-equations-and-potentials preview_or_optional_candidate]
The first says that \mathcal B is closed:
      d\mathcal B=0.
      On a small rectangular box, closed 2-forms are exact, as the construction
      below shows. Thus locally we can write
      \boxed{\mathcal B=d\mathcal A}
      for some 1-form \mathcal A, called the magnetic vector potential.

[588-602 p c19s4-local-magnetic-potential preview_or_optional_candidate]
For completeness, write \mathcal B=P\,dy\wedge dz+Q\,dz\wedge dx+R\,dx\wedge dy
      on a box with fixed interior coordinates x_0,y_0; time is a parameter.
      Closedness is P_x+Q_y+R_z=0. Set
      
        A_1=0,\qquad A_2(x,y,z)\amp=\int_{x_0}^x R(s,y,z)\,ds,
        A_3(x,y,z)\amp=-\int_{x_0}^x Q(s,y,z)\,ds+\int_{y_0}^y P(x_0,s,z)\,ds.
      
      The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus gives -(A_3)_x=Q and (A_2)_x=R.
      Differentiating the other integrals as in  gives
      (A_3)_y-(A_2)_z=P(x_0,y,z)-\int_{x_0}^x(Q_y+R_z)(s,y,z)\,ds=P(x,y,z).
      Consequently \mathcal A=A_1\,dx+A_2\,dy+A_3\,dz satisfies
      d\mathcal A=\mathcal B. This is a local construction; it makes no claim
      about one potential on a whole domain with holes.

[604-615 p c19s4-subsec-homogeneous-equations-and-potentials preview_or_optional_candidate]
Then Faraday's law becomes
      d\mathcal E+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(d\mathcal A)=0.
      Because d and \partial/\partial t commute,
      d(\mathcal E+\mathcal A_t)=0.
      A closed 1-form on this box is exact by
      . So we can write
      \mathcal E+\mathcal A_t=-d\varphi,
      or
      \boxed{\mathcal E=-d\varphi-\mathcal A_t.}
      The function \varphi is the electric scalar potential.

[617-622 p c19s4-subsec-homogeneous-equations-and-potentials preview_or_optional_candidate]
These formulas are not needed to use Maxwell's equations, but they show another place where
      the identity
      d^2=0
      organizes the theory.

[624-631 p c19s4-subsec-homogeneous-equations-and-potentials preview_or_optional_candidate]
There is a warning from : closed does not always mean
      globally exact. Missing points, holes, and topology can matter. The equation
      d\mathcal B=0
      is local. Writing
      \mathcal B=d\mathcal A
      globally may require assumptions about the region.

[635-635 title c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
What changes in space-time

[637-640 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
So far, time has been a parameter. At each time t, we wrote forms on ordinary space:
      \mathcal E,\quad \mathcal B,\quad \mathcal D,\quad \mathcal H.

[642-646 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
But Maxwell's equations are really space-time equations. Time is not merely a label outside
      the geometry. It joins the coordinates:
      (t,x,y,z).

[648-651 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
Let d_4 denote the exterior derivative in space-time. It differentiates with respect
      to t, x, y, and z.

[653-664 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
This extension uses the same coordinate rule as in space. Add the coordinate covector
      dt, and set d_4f=f_t\,dt+f_x\,dx+f_y\,dy+f_z\,dz.
      On each term f\,d\xi_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\xi_k, where every
      \xi_i is one of t,x,y,z, define
      d_4(f\,d\xi_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\xi_k)
      =d_4f\wedge d\xi_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\xi_k,
      and add the results. Swapping coordinate covectors changes sign, and a repeated
      coordinate factor gives zero. In d_4^2, every mixed partial appears twice
      with opposite wedge orders; continuous second partials therefore give d_4^2=0,
      by the same calculation as in .

[666-678 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
With one sign convention, define the electromagnetic field 2-form
      \boxed{F=\mathcal B+\mathcal E\wedge dt.}
      Then
      \begin{aligned} d_4F &= d\mathcal B+dt\wedge\left(\mathcal B_t+d\mathcal E\right). \end{aligned}
      Therefore
      \boxed{d_4F=0}
      is exactly the pair
      d\mathcal B=0,
      d\mathcal E+\mathcal B_t=0.
      So the no-magnetic-monopoles law and Faraday's law combine into one space-time equation:
      \boxed{d_4F=0.}

[680-695 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
The other pair also combines. Define
      \boxed{G=\mathcal D-\mathcal H\wedge dt.}
      Then
      \begin{aligned} d_4G &= d\mathcal D+dt\wedge(\mathcal D_t-d\mathcal H). \end{aligned}
      Using
      d\mathcal D=\mathcal Q
      and
      d\mathcal H-\mathcal D_t=\mathcal J,
      we get
      \boxed{d_4G=\mathcal Q-dt\wedge\mathcal J.}
      The right side is the space-time current 3-form:
      \boxed{\mathscr J=\mathcal Q-dt\wedge\mathcal J.}
      So the second pair becomes
      \boxed{d_4G=\mathscr J.}

[697-705 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
Now apply d_4 again:
      d_4(d_4G)=d_4\mathscr J.
      Since
      d_4^2=0,
      we get
      \boxed{d_4\mathscr J=0.}
      That is charge conservation in space-time.

[707-711 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
The space-time form of Maxwell's equations is therefore
      \boxed{d_4F=0,}
      \boxed{d_4G=\mathscr J.}

[713-713 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
This is the gateway.

[716-716 title checkpoint-19-4-gateway-elegance preview_or_optional_candidate]
What did d_4 buy us?

[718-724 p checkpoint-19-4-gateway-elegance preview_or_optional_candidate]
Four separate vector equations collapsed into just d_4F=0 and
          d_4G=\mathscr J. Beyond looking tidier, what does this compression actually
          tell us? Why is it meaningful that the homogeneous pair becomes a single
          statement that F is closed, and what does that suggest about how \mathcal E
          and \mathcal B are related?

[727-732 p checkpoint-19-4-gateway-elegance hint]
A single equation d_4F=0 treats \mathcal E and \mathcal B as two
          faces of one space-time form. Ask what changing your point of view (mixing t with
          a space coordinate) would then do to that one object, and why a frame-independent
          statement is more honest than four frame-dependent ones.

[736-741 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
Electric and magnetic fields are no longer two unrelated vector fields. They are pieces of
      space-time forms. The equations are no longer four separate vector identities. They are two
      exterior-derivative equations, with conservation following from
      d_4^2=0.

[743-748 p c19s4-subsec-what-changes-in-space-time preview_or_optional_candidate]
A later course explains how the geometry of space-time relates F and G. That
      relationship uses the metric of space-time, and the operation that appears there is the
      Hodge star. We do not need it here. The point for this book is simpler and more important:
      \boxed{\text{the language of forms is the natural language of Maxwell's equations.}}

[752-752 title c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
The book's main arc, seen from the end

[754-758 p c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
The book began with a displacement:
      (3,4).
      It was only an instruction to move.

[760-766 p c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
Then we learned that vectors could measure length, area, and volume. The wedge product
      remembered orientation:
      dx\wedge dy,\qquad dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.
      Functions became 0-forms. Work integrands became 1-forms. Flux integrands
      became 2-forms. Densities became 3-forms.

[768-779 p c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
The exterior derivative connected them:
      0\text{-forms}\xrightarrow{d}1\text{-forms}\xrightarrow{d}2\text{-forms}\xrightarrow{d}3\text{-forms}.
      In vector language, those arrows became
      \nabla,\qquad \nabla\times,\qquad \nabla\cdot.
      The identity
      d^2=0
      became the quiet reason behind
      \nabla\times\nabla f=\mathbf 0
      and
      \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf F)=0.

[781-785 p c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
Stokes' theorem then said:
      \int_{\partial M}\omega=\int_M d\omega.
      Boundary measurements and interior derivatives were two views of the same thing.

[787-791 p c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
Finally, conservation laws added time. The continuity equation said:
      \text{time change of stored amount}+\text{spatial derivative of flux}=\text{source}.
      Heat, waves, and equilibrium showed how physical assumptions choose fluxes and forces.

[793-797 p c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
Maxwell's equations show the whole structure at once. Electric circulation, magnetic flux,
      charge density, current flux, Stokes' theorem, d, and d^2=0 all appear in one
      system.

[799-799 p c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
So the final lesson is not a formula. It is a way of seeing.

[801-807 md c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
      &\text{Local derivatives describe what happens inside.}\\
      &\text{Boundary integrals record what crosses or circulates outside.}\\
      &\text{Differential forms keep the dimensions and orientations honest.}
      \end{aligned}}

[809-812 p c19s4-subsec-main-arc-from-the-end preview_or_optional_candidate]
That is the promise of multivariable calculus fulfilled: from tiny changes near one point to
      global laws over curves, surfaces, solids, and finally space-time.
