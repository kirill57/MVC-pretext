[5-5 title sec-appF-divergence-theorem default_required]
Divergence theorem

[8-8 title appF-div-statement-in-forms default_required]
Statement in forms

[10-21 p appF-div-statement-in-forms default_required]
Let
      
        \Phi_{\mathbf F}
        =
        P\,dy\wedge dz
        +
        Q\,dz\wedge dx
        +
        R\,dx\wedge dy.

[23-32 p appF-div-statement-in-forms default_required]
If E is a solid with the standard orientation and outward boundary \partial E, then
      
        \boxed{
        \int_{\partial E}\Phi_{\mathbf F}
        =
        \int_E d\Phi_{\mathbf F}.
        }

[34-50 p appF-div-statement-in-forms default_required]
Since
      
        d\Phi_{\mathbf F}
        =
        (P_x+Q_y+R_z)\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz,
      
      this becomes
      
        \boxed{
        \int_{\partial E}\Phi_{\mathbf F}
        =
        \iiint_E
        (P_x+Q_y+R_z)\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.
        }

[54-54 title appF-div-classical-statement default_required]
Classical statement

[56-66 p appF-div-classical-statement default_required]
\boxed{
        \iint_{\partial E}
        \mathbf F\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}\,dS
        =
        \iiint_E
        \nabla\cdot\mathbf F\,dV.
        }

[70-70 title appF-div-hypotheses default_required]
Hypotheses

[72-80 p appF-div-hypotheses default_required]
\begin{array}{ll}
        1. \amp E\text{ is a bounded solid with piecewise smooth boundary.}\\
        2. \amp \partial E\text{ is oriented outward.}\\
        3. \amp \mathbf F\text{ has continuous first partial derivatives on a region containing }E.
        \end{array}

[84-84 title appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Proof idea

[86-86 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
First prove the theorem for a small rectangular box.

[88-93 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Flux through the two x-faces gives approximately
      
        P_x\,\Delta x\,\Delta y\,\Delta z.

[95-100 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Flux through the two y-faces gives approximately
      
        Q_y\,\Delta x\,\Delta y\,\Delta z.

[102-107 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Flux through the two z-faces gives approximately
      
        R_z\,\Delta x\,\Delta y\,\Delta z.

[109-116 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Together:
      
        \text{outward flux}
        \approx
        (P_x+Q_y+R_z)\,\Delta V.

[118-118 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Now subdivide a solid into many small boxes.

[120-123 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Flux through interior faces cancels because each shared face is outward for one box and
      inward for the neighboring box.

[125-125 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Only the outer boundary remains.

[127-127 p appF-div-proof-idea default_required]
Let the box sizes shrink.

[131-131 title appF-div-dependencies default_required]
Dependencies

[133-143 p appF-div-dependencies default_required]
\begin{array}{ll}
        1. \amp \text{Flux forms.}\\
        2. \amp \text{Exterior derivative of a }2\text{-form.}\\
        3. \amp \text{Triple integrals.}\\
        4. \amp \text{Cancellation across shared faces.}\\
        5. \amp \text{Single-variable FTC along coordinate directions.}
        \end{array}

[147-147 title appF-div-what-can-fail default_required]
What can fail

[149-149 p appF-div-what-can-fail default_required]
The field must be defined inside the solid, not only on the boundary.

[151-158 p appF-div-what-can-fail default_required]
Example:
      
        \mathbf F(x,y,z)
        =
        \frac{(x,y,z)}{(x^2+y^2+z^2)^{3/2}}.

[160-160 p appF-div-what-can-fail default_required]
This field is not defined at the origin.

[162-167 p appF-div-what-can-fail default_required]
Away from the origin,
      
        \nabla\cdot\mathbf F=0.

[169-182 p appF-div-what-can-fail default_required]
But on the unit sphere,
      
        \mathbf F=\widehat{\mathbf n},
      
      so
      
        \iint_S \mathbf F\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}\,dS
        =
        \iint_S 1\,dS
        =
        4\pi.

[184-187 p appF-div-what-can-fail default_required]
The divergence theorem cannot be applied to the unit ball because the field is singular
      at the origin.

[191-191 title appF-div-moral default_required]
Moral

[193-197 p appF-div-moral default_required]
The divergence theorem is a flux bookkeeping theorem. What leaves through the
      boundary is accounted for by sources and sinks inside, provided the field is smooth
      throughout the solid.
