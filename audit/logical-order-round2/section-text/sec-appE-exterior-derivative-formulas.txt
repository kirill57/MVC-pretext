[5-5 title sec-appE-exterior-derivative-formulas default_required]
Exterior derivative formulas

[8-8 title appE-extd-main-idea default_required]
Main idea

[10-13 p appE-extd-main-idea default_required]
The exterior derivative raises degree by one:
      \boxed{d:\text{\(k\)-forms}\longrightarrow \text{\((k+1)\)-forms}.}

[15-21 md appE-extd-main-idea default_required]
\text{Input} \amp \text{Output}
      0\text{-form} \amp 1\text{-form}
      1\text{-form} \amp 2\text{-form}
      2\text{-form} \amp 3\text{-form}
      3\text{-form} \amp 0\text{ in }\mathbb R^3

[25-25 title appE-extd-derivative-of-a-0-form default_required]
Derivative of a 0-form

[27-30 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-0-form default_required]
In the plane:
      \boxed{df=f_x\,dx+f_y\,dy.}

[32-35 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-0-form default_required]
In space:
      \boxed{df=f_x\,dx+f_y\,dy+f_z\,dz.}

[37-37 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-0-form default_required]
This corresponds to the gradient.

[41-41 title appE-extd-derivative-of-a-plane-1-form default_required]
Derivative of a plane 1-form

[43-46 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-plane-1-form default_required]
Let
      \omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy.

[48-51 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-plane-1-form default_required]
Then
      \boxed{d\omega=(Q_x-P_y)\,dx\wedge dy.}

[53-53 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-plane-1-form default_required]
This is scalar curl times signed area.

[57-57 title appE-extd-derivative-of-a-space-1-form default_required]
Derivative of a space 1-form

[59-62 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-space-1-form default_required]
Let
      \omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz.

[64-75 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-space-1-form default_required]
Then
      \boxed{\begin{aligned}
      d\omega
      &=
      (R_y-Q_z)\,dy\wedge dz\\
      &\quad+
      (P_z-R_x)\,dz\wedge dx\\
      &\quad+
      (Q_x-P_y)\,dx\wedge dy.
      \end{aligned}}

[77-77 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-space-1-form default_required]
This corresponds to curl.

[81-81 title appE-extd-derivative-of-a-space-2-form default_required]
Derivative of a space 2-form

[83-86 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-space-2-form default_required]
Let
      \Phi=P\,dy\wedge dz+Q\,dz\wedge dx+R\,dx\wedge dy.

[88-91 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-space-2-form default_required]
Then
      \boxed{d\Phi=(P_x+Q_y+R_z)\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.}

[93-93 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-space-2-form default_required]
This corresponds to divergence.

[97-97 title appE-extd-derivative-of-a-3-form-in-r3 default_required]
Derivative of a 3-form in \mathbb R^3

[99-105 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-3-form-in-r3 default_required]
If
      \Omega=f\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz,
      then
      \boxed{d\Omega=0}
      in \mathbb R^3.

[107-107 p appE-extd-derivative-of-a-3-form-in-r3 default_required]
There are no nonzero 4-forms in three-dimensional space.

[111-111 title appE-extd-product-rule default_required]
Product rule

[113-116 p appE-extd-product-rule default_required]
If \alpha is a k-form, then
      \boxed{d(\alpha\wedge\beta)=d\alpha\wedge\beta+(-1)^k\alpha\wedge d\beta.}

[118-121 p appE-extd-product-rule default_required]
Special case for a function f:
      \boxed{d(f\alpha)=df\wedge\alpha+f\,d\alpha.}

[125-125 title appE-extd-exterior-derivative-squared default_required]
Exterior derivative squared

[127-129 p appE-extd-exterior-derivative-squared default_required]
\boxed{d(d\omega)=0.}

[131-134 p appE-extd-exterior-derivative-squared default_required]
Usually written:
      \boxed{d^2=0.}

[136-139 p appE-extd-exterior-derivative-squared default_required]
Classical meanings:
      \boxed{\operatorname{curl}(\nabla f)=\mathbf 0.}

[141-143 p appE-extd-exterior-derivative-squared default_required]
\boxed{\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{curl}\mathbf F)=0.}

[147-147 title appE-extd-closed-and-exact-forms default_required]
Closed and exact forms

[149-152 p appE-extd-closed-and-exact-forms default_required]
A form \omega is closed if
      \boxed{d\omega=0.}

[154-158 p appE-extd-closed-and-exact-forms default_required]
A form \omega is exact if
      \boxed{\omega=d\eta}
      for some form \eta.

[160-163 p appE-extd-closed-and-exact-forms default_required]
Exact forms are always closed:
      \boxed{\omega=d\eta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d\omega=d^2\eta=0.}

[165-168 p appE-extd-closed-and-exact-forms default_required]
The converse is not always true. Holes in the domain can prevent a closed form from
      being exact.

[172-172 title appE-extd-common-warnings default_required]
Common warnings

[175-175 p appE-extd-common-warnings default_required]
Partial derivative notation depends on the coordinate variables being used.

[177-177 p appE-extd-common-warnings default_required]
The exterior derivative does not depend on a metric.

[179-182 p appE-extd-common-warnings default_required]
The gradient, curl, and divergence interpretations use Euclidean structure to translate
        forms back into vectors.
