[5-5 title sec-appF-equality-of-mixed-partials default_required]
Equality of mixed partials

[8-8 title appF-mixed-statement default_required]
Statement

[10-14 p appF-mixed-statement default_required]
If f_{xy} and f_{yx} are continuous near a point, then
      \boxed{f_{xy}=f_{yx}}
      at that point.

[16-19 p appF-mixed-statement default_required]
More generally, if the relevant higher partial derivatives are continuous, then the order
      of differentiation can be switched.

[23-23 title appF-mixed-hypotheses default_required]
Hypotheses

[25-30 p appF-mixed-hypotheses default_required]
1. \amp f_{xy}\text{ and }f_{yx}\text{ exist near the point.}
        2. \amp f_{xy}\text{ and }f_{yx}\text{ are continuous near the point.}

[34-34 title appF-mixed-proof-idea default_required]
Proof idea

[36-39 p appF-mixed-proof-idea default_required]
Measure the net change of f around a small rectangle:
      [a,a+h]\times[b,b+k].

[41-44 p appF-mixed-proof-idea default_required]
Form the corner difference
      \boxed{\Delta=f(a+h,b+k)-f(a+h,b)-f(a,b+k)+f(a,b).}

[46-46 p appF-mixed-proof-idea default_required]
Compute \Delta in two ways.

[48-51 p appF-mixed-proof-idea default_required]
First, take x-changes, then y-changes. This connects \Delta to
      f_{xy}.

[53-56 p appF-mixed-proof-idea default_required]
Second, take y-changes, then x-changes. This connects \Delta to
      f_{yx}.

[58-65 p appF-mixed-proof-idea default_required]
Both computations measure the same corner difference. After division by
      hk
      and letting
      h,k\to0,
      continuity forces
      f_{xy}(a,b)=f_{yx}(a,b).

[69-69 title appF-mixed-dependencies default_required]
Dependencies

[71-77 p appF-mixed-dependencies default_required]
1. \amp \text{Single-variable FTC or Mean Value Theorem.}
        2. \amp \text{Continuity of the mixed partial derivatives.}
        3. \amp \text{Rectangle difference quotients.}

[81-81 title appF-mixed-what-can-fail default_required]
What can fail

[83-83 p appF-mixed-what-can-fail default_required]
If the mixed partials are not continuous, they can differ.

[85-94 p appF-mixed-what-can-fail default_required]
Define
      
        f(x,y)=
        \begin{cases}
        \dfrac{xy(x^2-y^2)}{x^2+y^2}, \amp (x,y)\ne(0,0),\\
        0, \amp (x,y)=(0,0).
        \end{cases}

[96-101 p appF-mixed-what-can-fail default_required]
Then
      f_x(0,y)=-y,
      so
      f_{xy}(0,0)=-1.

[103-108 p appF-mixed-what-can-fail default_required]
Also,
      f_y(x,0)=x,
      so
      f_{yx}(0,0)=1.

[110-113 p appF-mixed-what-can-fail default_required]
Thus
      \boxed{f_{xy}(0,0)\ne f_{yx}(0,0).}

[117-117 title appF-mixed-moral default_required]
Moral

[119-123 p appF-mixed-moral default_required]
The equality of mixed partials is not only about the symbols xy and yx. It is
      about enough smoothness to make the small-rectangle measurement independent of the order in
      which it is taken.
