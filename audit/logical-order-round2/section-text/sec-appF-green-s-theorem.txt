[5-5 title sec-appF-green-s-theorem default_required]
Green's theorem

[8-8 title appF-green-statement-in-forms default_required]
Statement in forms

[10-13 p appF-green-statement-in-forms default_required]
Let
      \omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy.

[15-24 p appF-green-statement-in-forms default_required]
If R is a positively oriented plane region, then
      
      \boxed{
          \int_{\partial R}\omega
          =
          \int_R d\omega.
      }

[26-37 p appF-green-statement-in-forms default_required]
Since
      d\omega=(Q_x-P_y)\,dx\wedge dy,
      this is
      
      \boxed{
          \int_{\partial R}P\,dx+Q\,dy
          =
          \iint_R (Q_x-P_y)\,dx\wedge dy.
      }

[41-41 title appF-green-classical-statement default_required]
Classical statement

[43-59 p appF-green-classical-statement default_required]
For
      \mathbf F=(P,Q),
      
      \boxed{
          \oint_{\partial R}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r
          =
          \iint_R
          \left(
              \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}
              -
              \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}
          \right)
          dA.
      }

[63-63 title appF-green-hypotheses default_required]
Hypotheses

[65-71 p appF-green-hypotheses default_required]
1. \amp R\text{ is a bounded plane region with piecewise smooth boundary.}
        2. \amp \partial R\text{ has positive orientation.}
        3. \amp P\text{ and }Q\text{ have continuous first partial derivatives on a region containing }R.

[73-80 p appF-green-hypotheses default_required]
Positive orientation means:
      
      \boxed{
          \text{walk so the region stays on your left.}
      }

[84-84 title appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
Proof idea

[86-86 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
First prove the theorem for one small rectangle.

[88-92 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
For
      R=[a,a+h]\times[b,b+k],
      the line integral around the rectangle compares opposite sides.

[94-97 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
Horizontal sides produce the term
      -P_y\,hk.

[99-102 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
Vertical sides produce the term
      Q_x\,hk.

[104-111 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
Together:
      
          \int_{\partial R}P\,dx+Q\,dy
          \approx
          (Q_x-P_y)hk.

[113-113 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
Then subdivide a larger region into many small rectangles.

[115-115 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
Interior edges are walked twice in opposite directions, so their contributions cancel.

[117-117 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
Only the outer boundary remains.

[119-119 p appF-green-proof-idea default_required]
Let the mesh size go to zero.

[123-123 title appF-green-dependencies default_required]
Dependencies

[125-132 p appF-green-dependencies default_required]
1. \amp \text{Line integrals of }1\text{-forms.}
        2. \amp \text{Exterior derivative }d(P\,dx+Q\,dy).
        3. \amp \text{Fubini's theorem.}
        4. \amp \text{Cancellation of oppositely oriented interior edges.}

[136-136 title appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
What can fail

[138-138 p appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
Orientation matters.

[140-140 p appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
If the boundary is walked clockwise instead of counterclockwise, the sign changes.

[142-142 p appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
The field must be defined on the whole region.

[144-152 p appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
Example:
      
          \omega=
          \frac{-y}{x^2+y^2}\,dx
          +
          \frac{x}{x^2+y^2}\,dy.

[154-157 p appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
This form is defined on
      \mathbb R^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}.

[159-162 p appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
On that punctured plane,
      d\omega=0.

[164-167 p appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
But around the unit circle,
      \int_{C}\omega=2\pi.

[169-172 p appF-green-what-can-fail default_required]
Green's theorem cannot be applied to the disk bounded by C, because \omega is
      not defined at the origin.

[176-176 title appF-green-moral default_required]
Moral

[178-181 p appF-green-moral default_required]
Green's theorem is a cancellation theorem. Local rotation inside a region adds up to
      circulation around the boundary, as long as the field is smooth on the whole region.
