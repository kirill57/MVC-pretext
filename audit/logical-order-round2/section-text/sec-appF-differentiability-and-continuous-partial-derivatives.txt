[3-3 title sec-appF-differentiability-and-continuous-partial-derivatives default_required]
Differentiability and continuous partial derivatives

[6-6 title appF-diff-statement default_required]
Statement

[8-14 p appF-diff-statement default_required]
Let
      f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R.
      If the first partial derivatives of f exist in a neighborhood of \mathbf p
      and are continuous at \mathbf p, then f is differentiable at
      \mathbf p.

[16-34 p appF-diff-statement default_required]
In two variables:
      \boxed{
    f(a+h,b+k)
    =
    f(a,b)
    +
    f_x(a,b)h
    +
    f_y(a,b)k
    +
    \text{error}
}
      where
      \boxed{
    \frac{\text{error}}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2}}\to0
    \quad\text{as }(h,k)\to(0,0).
}

[38-38 title appF-diff-differential-form default_required]
Differential form

[40-46 p appF-diff-differential-form default_required]
\boxed{
    df_{\mathbf p}
    =
    f_{x_1}(\mathbf p)\,dx_1+\cdots+f_{x_n}(\mathbf p)\,dx_n.
}

[48-53 p appF-diff-differential-form default_required]
In two variables:
      \boxed{
    df_{(a,b)}=f_x(a,b)\,dx+f_y(a,b)\,dy.
}

[57-57 title appF-diff-hypotheses default_required]
Hypotheses

[59-64 p appF-diff-hypotheses default_required]
1. \amp \text{The partial derivatives exist near the point.}
        2. \amp \text{The partial derivatives are continuous at the point.}

[66-68 p appF-diff-hypotheses default_required]
The theorem is a sufficient condition, not a necessary condition.

[70-72 p appF-diff-hypotheses default_required]
A function can be differentiable even if its partial derivatives are not continuous.

[76-76 title appF-diff-proof-idea-two-variables default_required]
Proof idea in two variables

[78-85 p appF-diff-proof-idea-two-variables default_required]
Move from
      (a,b)
      to
      (a+h,b+k)
      in two coordinate steps:
      (a,b)\to(a+h,b)\to(a+h,b+k).

[87-96 p appF-diff-proof-idea-two-variables default_required]
Then
      
        f(a+h,b+k)-f(a,b)
        \amp=
        \bigl[f(a+h,b+k)-f(a+h,b)\bigr]
        \amp\quad+
        \bigl[f(a+h,b)-f(a,b)\bigr].

[98-107 p appF-diff-proof-idea-two-variables default_required]
Apply the one-variable Mean Value Theorem to each
      coordinate slice:
      f(a+h,b)-f(a,b)
    \approx
    f_x(a,b)h,
      f(a+h,b+k)-f(a+h,b)
    \approx
    f_y(a,b)k.

[109-122 p appF-diff-proof-idea-two-variables default_required]
Continuity of f_x and f_y makes the approximation uniform as
      (h,k)\to(0,0).
      More precisely, the Mean Value Theorem writes the two increments as
      h f_x(a+\theta h,b) and k f_y(a+h,b+\tau k) for some
      0\lt\theta,\tau\lt1 (a zero increment contributes zero). Continuity at
      (a,b) makes each partial differ from its value there by less than
      \varepsilon when \sqrt{h^2+k^2} is small enough. Hence the
      error after subtracting the proposed linear part has magnitude at most
      \varepsilon(|h|+|k|)\le\sqrt2\varepsilon\sqrt{h^2+k^2}.
      Dividing by the increment length proves the required limit. In n
      variables, use n coordinate steps and
      \sum_i|h_i|\le\sqrt n\,|\mathbf h|.

[124-129 p appF-diff-proof-idea-two-variables default_required]
So the first-order part is
      \boxed{
    f_x(a,b)h+f_y(a,b)k.
}

[133-133 title appF-diff-dependencies default_required]
Dependencies

[135-141 p appF-diff-dependencies default_required]
1. \amp \text{Single-variable Mean Value Theorem.}
        2. \amp \text{Continuity of partial derivatives.}
        3. \amp \text{Linear approximation in one variable.}

[145-145 title appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
What can fail

[147-149 p appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
Partial derivatives can exist without differentiability.

[151-158 p appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
Define
      f(x,y)=
    \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}, \amp (x,y)\ne(0,0),\\
    0, \amp (x,y)=(0,0).
    \end{cases}

[160-165 p appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
At the origin,
      f_x(0,0)=0,
    \qquad
    f_y(0,0)=0.

[167-170 p appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
If these partial derivatives gave a true differential, the linear approximation would be
      zero.

[172-177 p appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
But along the path
      (x,y)=(t,t),
      we get
      f(t,t)=\frac{t^2}{\sqrt{2t^2}}=\frac{|t|}{\sqrt2}.

[179-182 p appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
The distance to the origin is
      \sqrt{t^2+t^2}=\sqrt2\,|t|.

[184-188 p appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
The ratio is
      \frac{|t|/\sqrt2}{\sqrt2\,|t|}=\frac12,
      which does not go to 0.

[190-192 p appF-diff-what-can-fail default_required]
So f is not differentiable at the origin.

[196-196 title appF-diff-moral default_required]
Moral

[198-201 p appF-diff-moral default_required]
Coordinate slopes alone do not guarantee a true tangent plane. Continuous partial
      derivatives stitch the coordinate information into one linear approximation.
