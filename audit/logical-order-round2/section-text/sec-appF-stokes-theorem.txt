[5-5 title sec-appF-stokes-theorem default_required]
Stokes' theorem

[8-8 title appF-stokes-statement-in-forms default_required]
Statement in forms

[10-13 p appF-stokes-statement-in-forms default_required]
Let
      \omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz.

[15-18 p appF-stokes-statement-in-forms default_required]
If S is an oriented surface with oriented boundary \partial S, then
      \boxed{\int_{\partial S}\omega=\int_S d\omega.}

[22-22 title appF-stokes-classical-statement default_required]
Classical statement

[24-28 p appF-stokes-classical-statement default_required]
For
      \mathbf F=(P,Q,R),
      \boxed{\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}\,dS.}

[32-32 title appF-stokes-hypotheses default_required]
Hypotheses

[34-41 p appF-stokes-hypotheses default_required]
1. \amp S\text{ is a compact oriented piecewise smooth surface.}
        2. \amp \partial S\text{ has the boundary orientation induced by }S.
        3. \amp P,Q,R\text{ have continuous first partial derivatives near }S.
        4. \amp \omega\text{ is defined on a region containing }S.

[43-46 p appF-stokes-hypotheses default_required]
Boundary orientation is given by the right-hand rule:
      \boxed{\text{thumb points along the chosen normal, fingers curl along }\partial S.}

[50-50 title appF-stokes-proof-idea default_required]
Proof idea

[52-55 p appF-stokes-proof-idea default_required]
For a single regular patch, use a C^2 parametrization that is one-to-one in the interior and traces the boundary once:
      \mathbf r(u,v).

[57-60 p appF-stokes-proof-idea default_required]
Pull back the 1-form:
      \mathbf r^*\omega.

[62-64 p appF-stokes-proof-idea default_required]
Now \mathbf r^*\omega is a 1-form in the uv-plane.

[66-69 p appF-stokes-proof-idea default_required]
Apply Green's theorem in the parameter region:
      \int_{\partial D}\mathbf r^*\omega=\int_D d(\mathbf r^*\omega).

[71-74 p appF-stokes-proof-idea default_required]
Use the pullback identity, established by the coordinate calculation in the surface case:
      \boxed{d(\mathbf r^*\omega)=\mathbf r^*(d\omega).}

[76-79 p appF-stokes-proof-idea default_required]
This gives
      \int_{\partial S}\omega=\int_S d\omega.

[83-83 title appF-stokes-dependencies default_required]
Dependencies

[85-93 p appF-stokes-dependencies default_required]
1. \amp \text{Green's theorem.}
        2. \amp \text{Parametrized surfaces.}
        3. \amp \text{Pullbacks of forms.}
        4. \amp \text{Exterior derivative commutes with pullback.}
        5. \amp \text{Orientation from }\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v.

[97-97 title appF-stokes-what-can-fail default_required]
What can fail

[99-99 p appF-stokes-what-can-fail default_required]
Orientation matters.

[101-104 p appF-stokes-what-can-fail default_required]
If the surface normal is reversed, then the boundary orientation reverses and both
      sides change sign.

[106-106 p appF-stokes-what-can-fail default_required]
The field must be defined on the surface.

[108-108 p appF-stokes-what-can-fail default_required]
Singularities can break the theorem if they lie on the chosen surface.

[110-115 p appF-stokes-what-can-fail default_required]
If S has no boundary, then
      \partial S=\varnothing,
      so Stokes gives
      \boxed{\int_S d\omega=0.}

[117-121 p appF-stokes-what-can-fail default_required]
Classically:
      \boxed{\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n}\,dS=0}
      for a closed oriented surface, provided \mathbf F is smooth near S.

[125-125 title appF-stokes-moral default_required]
Moral

[127-130 p appF-stokes-moral default_required]
Stokes' theorem is Green's theorem on a curved surface. The pullback flattens the
      surface into a parameter region where Green's theorem can do the work.
