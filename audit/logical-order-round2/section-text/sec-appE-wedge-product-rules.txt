[5-5 title sec-appE-wedge-product-rules default_required]
Wedge product rules

[8-8 title appE-wedge-basic-antisymmetry default_required]
Basic antisymmetry

[10-12 p appE-wedge-basic-antisymmetry default_required]
\boxed{dx\wedge dy=-dy\wedge dx}

[14-16 p appE-wedge-basic-antisymmetry default_required]
\boxed{dy\wedge dz=-dz\wedge dy}

[18-20 p appE-wedge-basic-antisymmetry default_required]
\boxed{dz\wedge dx=-dx\wedge dz}

[22-22 p appE-wedge-basic-antisymmetry default_required]
Swapping two 1-form factors changes the sign.

[26-26 title appE-wedge-repeated-factors-vanish default_required]
Repeated factors vanish

[28-30 p appE-wedge-repeated-factors-vanish default_required]
\boxed{dx\wedge dx=0}

[32-34 p appE-wedge-repeated-factors-vanish default_required]
\boxed{dy\wedge dy=0}

[36-38 p appE-wedge-repeated-factors-vanish default_required]
\boxed{dz\wedge dz=0}

[40-40 p appE-wedge-repeated-factors-vanish default_required]
Any wedge product with a repeated differential is zero.

[44-44 title appE-wedge-positive-volume-order default_required]
Positive volume order

[46-49 p appE-wedge-positive-volume-order default_required]
\boxed{dx\wedge dy\wedge dz}
      is the positive volume form in the usual orientation of \mathbb R^3.

[51-54 p appE-wedge-positive-volume-order default_required]
Cyclic orders are positive:
      \boxed{dx\wedge dy\wedge dz=dy\wedge dz\wedge dx=dz\wedge dx\wedge dy.}

[56-59 p appE-wedge-positive-volume-order default_required]
Reversed cyclic orders are negative:
      \boxed{dx\wedge dz\wedge dy=dy\wedge dx\wedge dz=dz\wedge dy\wedge dx=-dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.}

[63-63 title appE-wedge-distributive-rules default_required]
Distributive rules

[65-67 p appE-wedge-distributive-rules default_required]
\boxed{\alpha\wedge(\beta+\gamma)=\alpha\wedge\beta+\alpha\wedge\gamma}

[69-71 p appE-wedge-distributive-rules default_required]
\boxed{(\alpha+\beta)\wedge\gamma=\alpha\wedge\gamma+\beta\wedge\gamma}

[73-76 p appE-wedge-distributive-rules default_required]
Functions can be moved through wedge products:
      \boxed{(f\alpha)\wedge\beta=f(\alpha\wedge\beta).}

[80-80 title appE-wedge-graded-sign-rule default_required]
Graded sign rule

[82-85 p appE-wedge-graded-sign-rule default_required]
If \alpha is a k-form and \beta is an \ell-form, then
      \boxed{\alpha\wedge\beta=(-1)^{k\ell}\beta\wedge\alpha.}

[87-90 p appE-wedge-graded-sign-rule default_required]
Examples:
      \boxed{\text{\(1\)-forms anticommute.}}

[92-94 p appE-wedge-graded-sign-rule default_required]
\boxed{\text{A \(1\)-form and a \(2\)-form commute.}}

[98-98 title appE-wedge-two-1-forms-in-the-plane default_required]
Two 1-forms in the plane

[100-103 p appE-wedge-two-1-forms-in-the-plane default_required]
Let
      \alpha=a\,dx+b\,dy, \qquad \beta=c\,dx+d\,dy.

[105-108 p appE-wedge-two-1-forms-in-the-plane default_required]
Then
      \boxed{\alpha\wedge\beta=(ad-bc)\,dx\wedge dy.}

[110-113 p appE-wedge-two-1-forms-in-the-plane default_required]
This is the determinant:
      \boxed{ad-bc=\det\begin{pmatrix} a&c\\ b&d \end{pmatrix}.}

[117-117 title appE-wedge-two-1-forms-in-space default_required]
Two 1-forms in space

[119-124 p appE-wedge-two-1-forms-in-space default_required]
Let
      \alpha=a_1\,dx+a_2\,dy+a_3\,dz,
      and
      \beta=b_1\,dx+b_2\,dy+b_3\,dz.

[126-131 p appE-wedge-two-1-forms-in-space default_required]
Then
      
        \boxed{\begin{aligned} \alpha\wedge\beta \amp= (a_2b_3-a_3b_2)\,dy\wedge dz\\ \amp\quad+ (a_3b_1-a_1b_3)\,dz\wedge dx\\ \amp\quad+ (a_1b_2-a_2b_1)\,dx\wedge dy. \end{aligned}}

[133-136 p appE-wedge-two-1-forms-in-space default_required]
The coefficients are the components of
      \boxed{\mathbf a\times\mathbf b.}

[140-140 title appE-wedge-three-1-forms-in-space default_required]
Three 1-forms in space

[142-147 p appE-wedge-three-1-forms-in-space default_required]
Let
      \alpha=a_1dx+a_2dy+a_3dz,
      \beta=b_1dx+b_2dy+b_3dz,
      \gamma=c_1dx+c_2dy+c_3dz.

[149-152 p appE-wedge-three-1-forms-in-space default_required]
Then
      \boxed{\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\gamma=\det\begin{pmatrix} a_1&b_1&c_1\\ a_2&b_2&c_2\\ a_3&b_3&c_3 \end{pmatrix} dx\wedge dy\wedge dz.}

[156-156 title appE-wedge-common-warnings default_required]
Common warnings

[157-157 p appE-wedge-common-warnings default_required]
The wedge product is not ordinary multiplication.

[159-159 p appE-wedge-common-warnings default_required]
Order matters.

[161-161 p appE-wedge-common-warnings default_required]
Repeated differentials vanish.

[163-166 p appE-wedge-common-warnings default_required]
In \mathbb R^3, every 4-form is zero because there are only three coordinate
      directions.
