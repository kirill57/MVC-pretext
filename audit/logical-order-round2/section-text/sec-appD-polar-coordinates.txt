[5-5 title sec-appD-polar-coordinates default_required]
Polar coordinates

[8-8 title appD-polar-conversion-formulas default_required]
Conversion formulas

[10-13 p appD-polar-conversion-formulas default_required]
Polar coordinates describe a point in the plane by radius and angle:
      \boxed{(r,\theta).}

[15-18 p appD-polar-conversion-formulas default_required]
Conversion to Cartesian:
      \boxed{x=r\cos\theta, \qquad y=r\sin\theta.}

[20-23 p appD-polar-conversion-formulas default_required]
Conversion to polar:
      \boxed{r^2=x^2+y^2.}

[25-28 p appD-polar-conversion-formulas default_required]
\boxed{\tan\theta=\frac yx}
      with quadrant care.

[32-32 title appD-polar-usual-ranges default_required]
Usual ranges

[34-37 p appD-polar-usual-ranges default_required]
Usually,
      \boxed{r\ge0, \qquad 0\le\theta\lt2\pi.}

[39-43 p appD-polar-usual-ranges default_required]
The angle is not unique:
      \boxed{(r,\theta)=(r,\theta+2\pi k)}
      for every integer k.

[45-48 p appD-polar-usual-ranges default_required]
If signed radii are allowed, then
      \boxed{(-r,\theta)=(r,\theta+\pi).}

[52-52 title appD-polar-unit-vectors default_required]
Unit vectors

[54-56 p appD-polar-unit-vectors default_required]
\boxed{\widehat{\mathbf e}_r=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)}

[58-60 p appD-polar-unit-vectors default_required]
\boxed{\widehat{\mathbf e}_\theta=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)}

[62-64 p appD-polar-unit-vectors default_required]
The vector \widehat{\mathbf e}_r points outward.

[66-69 p appD-polar-unit-vectors default_required]
The vector \widehat{\mathbf e}_\theta points in the direction of increasing
      \theta.

[73-73 title appD-polar-unit-vector-derivatives default_required]
Unit-vector derivatives

[75-77 p appD-polar-unit-vector-derivatives default_required]
\boxed{\frac{\partial \widehat{\mathbf e}_r}{\partial\theta} = \widehat{\mathbf e}_\theta}

[79-81 p appD-polar-unit-vector-derivatives default_required]
\boxed{\frac{\partial \widehat{\mathbf e}_\theta}{\partial\theta} = -\widehat{\mathbf e}_r}

[83-85 p appD-polar-unit-vector-derivatives default_required]
The polar unit vectors depend on position.

[89-89 title appD-polar-small-displacement default_required]
Small displacement

[91-93 p appD-polar-small-displacement default_required]
\boxed{d\mathbf r = \widehat{\mathbf e}_r\,dr + \widehat{\mathbf e}_\theta\,r\,d\theta.}

[97-97 title appD-polar-line-element default_required]
Line element

[99-101 p appD-polar-line-element default_required]
\boxed{ds^2=dr^2+r^2\,d\theta^2.}

[103-106 p appD-polar-line-element default_required]
So
      \boxed{ds=\sqrt{dr^2+r^2\,d\theta^2}.}

[108-111 p appD-polar-line-element default_required]
For a circle r=a:
      \boxed{ds=a\,|d\theta|.}

[113-118 p appD-polar-line-element default_required]
For a radial line \theta=\theta_0:
      \boxed{ds=|dr|.}
      Along an increasing-angle or increasing-radius parametrization, the absolute values
      may be omitted. In general, ds remains nonnegative when direction reverses.

[122-122 title appD-polar-area-element default_required]
Area element

[124-127 p appD-polar-area-element default_required]
Ordinary area:
      \boxed{dA=r\,dr\,d\theta.}

[129-132 p appD-polar-area-element default_required]
Oriented area:
      \boxed{dx\wedge dy = r\,dr\wedge d\theta.}

[136-136 title appD-polar-common-polar-curves default_required]
Common polar curves

[138-141 p appD-polar-common-polar-curves default_required]
Circle centered at origin:
      \boxed{r=a.}

[143-146 p appD-polar-common-polar-curves default_required]
Ray from the origin (for nonnegative r):
      \boxed{\theta=\theta_0.}

[148-151 p appD-polar-common-polar-curves default_required]
Vertical line:
      \boxed{x=a \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\cos\theta=a.}

[153-156 p appD-polar-common-polar-curves default_required]
Horizontal line:
      \boxed{y=b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\sin\theta=b.}

[158-161 p appD-polar-common-polar-curves default_required]
Circle through the origin with center on the x-axis:
      \boxed{r=2a\cos\theta.}

[163-166 p appD-polar-common-polar-curves default_required]
Circle through the origin with center on the y-axis:
      \boxed{r=2b\sin\theta.}

[170-170 title appD-polar-polar-integration default_required]
Polar integration

[172-175 p appD-polar-polar-integration default_required]
Area of a region:
      \boxed{\iint_R f(x,y)\,dA = \iint_{\widetilde R} f(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\,r\,dr\,d\theta.}

[177-180 p appD-polar-polar-integration default_required]
Area enclosed by r=f(\theta), traced once:
      \boxed{A=\frac12\int_\alpha^\beta f(\theta)^2\,d\theta.}

[184-184 title appD-polar-common-warnings default_required]
Common warnings

[185-189 p appD-polar-common-warnings default_required]
The formula
      \tan\theta=\frac yx
      does not determine the quadrant by itself.

[191-193 p appD-polar-common-warnings default_required]
The point at the origin has no unique angle.

[195-199 p appD-polar-common-warnings default_required]
The factor r in
      dA=r\,dr\,d\theta
      must not be dropped.

[201-204 p appD-polar-common-warnings default_required]
Formulas containing 1/r are singular at
      r=0.
