[5-5 title sec-16-pullbacks default_required]
Pullbacks

[7-12 p sec-16-pullbacks default_required]
ended with a practical problem. A form may be
    written in x,y,z-space, but the actual calculation may happen in a parameter
    domain. A curve uses a parameter t. A surface uses parameters u,v. A
    change of coordinates may use r,\theta,z or \rho,\phi,\theta.

[14-14 p sec-16-pullbacks default_required]
So we need one operation that answers this question:

[16-16 md sec-16-pullbacks default_required]
\text{How does a form look after we substitute the parametrization?}

[18-22 p sec-16-pullbacks default_required]
That operation is called the pullback. The name sounds abstract, but the
    idea is ordinary substitution, done carefully enough to remember dx,
    dy, dz, and wedge products.

[25-25 title c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
Substitution as the pullback idea

[27-34 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
In the substitution calculations below, an equation such as
      dx=x_u\,du+x_v\,dv abbreviates
      T^*(dx)=d(x\circ T), where the x on the right is the target coordinate
      function. The coordinate covector dx lives in the target; its pullback lives
      in the parameter domain. These equations identify their measurements after substitution,
      not covectors on two different spaces without a map between them.

[36-36 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
Start with a change of coordinates in the plane:

[38-38 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
T(u,v)=(x,y)=(u+v,\;2u-v).

[40-40 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
A point in the (u,v)-plane is sent to a point in the (x,y)-plane.

[42-42 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
Now take the 1-form

[44-44 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
\omega=x\,dy+y\,dx

[46-46 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
in the (x,y)-plane. We want to write this form in the (u,v)-coordinates.

[48-48 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
First substitute the coordinate functions:

[50-50 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
x=u+v, \qquad y=2u-v.

[52-52 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
Then substitute their differentials:

[54-54 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
dx=d(u+v)=du+dv,

[56-56 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
and

[58-58 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
dy=d(2u-v)=2\,du-dv.

[60-60 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
Therefore

[62-65 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
\omega \amp= x\,dy+y\,dx
      \amp\longmapsto (u+v)(2\,du-dv)+(2u-v)(du+dv).

[67-67 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
Expand:

[69-71 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
(u+v)(2\,du-dv) \amp= (2u+2v)\,du+(-u-v)\,dv,

[73-73 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
and

[75-77 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
(2u-v)(du+dv) \amp= (2u-v)\,du+(2u-v)\,dv.

[79-79 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
So

[81-81 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
\boxed{T^*\omega=(4u+v)\,du+(u-2v)\,dv.}

[83-83 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
The star in

[85-85 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
T^*\omega

[87-90 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
means pull \omega back through T. The original form lived in the
      (x,y)-world. The pulled-back form lives in the (u,v)-world.

[93-93 title c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
Pullback, coordinate version

[95-95 p c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
Let

[96-96 md c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
T(u,v)=(x(u,v),y(u,v))

[97-100 p c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
be a smooth map into the plane. To pull back a form from the (x,y)-plane to
          the (u,v)-plane, use the substitutions

[101-101 md c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
x=x(u,v), \qquad y=y(u,v),

[102-102 p c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
and

[103-103 md c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
dx=x_u\,du+x_v\,dv, \qquad dy=y_u\,du+y_v\,dv.

[104-104 p c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
The resulting form is called the pullback and is written

[105-105 md c16s5-def-pullback-coordinate default_required]
T^*\omega.

[109-109 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
A function is a 0-form, so its pullback is just ordinary composition:

[111-111 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
\boxed{T^*f=f\circ T.}

[113-113 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
For example, if

[115-115 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
f(x,y)=x^2+y,

[117-117 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
then

[119-122 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
T^*f \amp= f(u+v,2u-v)
      \amp= (u+v)^2+2u-v.

[124-127 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
For 1-forms, the pullback remembers the differentials. For 2-forms, it
      also remembers the wedge product.

[130-130 title c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
Pulling back an area form

[132-132 p c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
Let

[133-133 md c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
T(u,v)=(x,y)=(u+v,\;2u-v).

[134-134 p c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
Then

[135-135 md c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
dx=du+dv, \qquad dy=2\,du-dv.

[136-136 p c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
Pull back the basic area form:

[137-140 md c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
T^*(dx\wedge dy) \amp= (du+dv)\wedge(2\,du-dv)
          \amp= du\wedge 2\,du - du\wedge dv + dv\wedge 2\,du - dv\wedge dv.

[141-141 p c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
The repeated factors vanish:

[142-142 md c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
du\wedge du=0, \qquad dv\wedge dv=0.

[143-143 p c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
Also,

[144-144 md c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
dv\wedge du=-du\wedge dv.

[145-145 p c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
So

[146-149 md c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
T^*(dx\wedge dy) \amp= -du\wedge dv + 2(-du\wedge dv)
          \amp= -3\,du\wedge dv.

[150-150 p c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
Thus

[151-151 md c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
\boxed{T^*(dx\wedge dy)=-3\,du\wedge dv.}

[152-155 p c16s5-ex-pullback-area-form default_required]
The map multiplies signed area by -3. The negative sign says it reverses
          orientation.

[159-162 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
This example is the pullback version of the Jacobian determinant. We will return to
      that connection at the end of the section.

[164-164 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
The pullback obeys the algebra of forms:

[166-166 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
\boxed{T^*(\alpha+\beta)=T^*\alpha+T^*\beta,}

[168-168 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
\boxed{T^*(f\alpha)=(T^*f)(T^*\alpha),}

[170-170 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
and

[172-172 md c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
\boxed{T^*(\alpha\wedge\beta)=T^*\alpha\wedge T^*\beta.}

[174-177 p c16s5-subsec-substitution-pullback-idea default_required]
These rules are not extra tricks. They say that after substitution, we still add,
      multiply by functions, and wedge exactly as before.

[179-179 title checkpoint-16-5-why-pullback-back default_required]
Why does the form move backward?

[181-186 p checkpoint-16-5-why-pullback-back default_required]
The map T sends points forward, from the (u,v)-world to the
          (x,y)-world, yet T^* carries the form \omega the other way,
          from (x,y) back to (u,v). Why is it natural for a form to travel
          opposite to the points, rather than alongside them?

[189-193 p checkpoint-16-5-why-pullback-back hint]
Think about what a form does for a living: it waits to be fed a coordinate
          direction and report a number. If you only know how to build directions in the
          (u,v)-world, which world must the form end up living in so it can read them?

[199-199 title c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
Pulling back 1-forms to curves

[201-201 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
A curve is a map from a parameter interval into space:

[203-203 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
\mathbf r(t)=(x(t),y(t),z(t)).

[205-208 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
Pulling a 1-form back to the curve turns it into a 1-form in the single
      variable t. That means it becomes something times dt.

[210-210 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
Let

[212-212 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
\omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz.

[214-214 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
Along the curve,

[216-216 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
x=x(t), \qquad y=y(t), \qquad z=z(t).

[218-218 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
So

[220-220 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
dx=x'(t)\,dt, \qquad dy=y'(t)\,dt, \qquad dz=z'(t)\,dt.

[222-222 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
Therefore

[224-224 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
\boxed{\mathbf r^*\omega = \bigl[ P(\mathbf r(t))x'(t) + Q(\mathbf r(t))y'(t) + R(\mathbf r(t))z'(t) \bigr]\,dt.}

[226-229 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
This is exactly the line-integral integrand from earlier chapters. The pullback is the
      operation that produces it.

[232-232 title c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
A form pulled back to a parabola

[234-234 p c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
Let

[235-235 md c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
\omega=(x+y)\,dx+x\,dy

[236-236 p c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
in the plane, and let

[237-237 md c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
\mathbf r(t)=(t,t^2), \qquad 0\le t\le2.

[238-238 p c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
Then

[239-239 md c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
x=t, \qquad y=t^2,

[240-240 p c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
and

[241-241 md c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
dx=dt, \qquad dy=2t\,dt.

[242-242 p c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
So

[243-246 md c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
\mathbf r^*\omega \amp= (t+t^2)\,dt+t(2t\,dt)
          \amp= (t+3t^2)\,dt.

[247-247 p c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
Thus

[248-248 md c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
\boxed{\mathbf r^*\omega=(t+3t^2)\,dt.}

[249-249 p c16s5-ex-form-pulled-back-parabola default_required]
The form is now ready to be integrated over the parameter interval.

[254-254 title c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
A helix measuring rotation and height

[256-256 p c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
Let

[257-257 md c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
\omega=-y\,dx+x\,dy+dz

[258-258 p c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
and let

[259-259 md c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
\mathbf r(t)=(\cos t,\sin t,t).

[260-260 p c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
Then

[261-261 md c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
dx=-\sin t\,dt, \qquad dy=\cos t\,dt, \qquad dz=dt.

[262-262 p c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
Also,

[263-263 md c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
x=\cos t, \qquad y=\sin t.

[264-264 p c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
Therefore

[265-269 md c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
\mathbf r^*\omega \amp= -\sin t(-\sin t\,dt) + \cos t(\cos t\,dt) + dt
          \amp= (\sin^2t+\cos^2t+1)\,dt
          \amp= 2\,dt.

[270-273 p c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
The rotational part contributes 1\,dt, and the vertical rise contributes
          another 1\,dt. Along this helix,

[274-274 md c16s5-ex-helix-rotation-height default_required]
\boxed{\mathbf r^*\omega=2\,dt.}

[278-281 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-1forms-curves default_required]
The curve supplies the tangent direction. The pullback asks the form to measure that
      direction and then rewrites the answer in the parameter variable.

[285-285 title c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
Pulling back 2-forms to surfaces

[287-287 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
A surface patch is described by two parameters:

[289-289 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\mathbf r(u,v)=(x(u,v),y(u,v),z(u,v)).

[291-291 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
A 2-form in space has the shape

[293-293 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\Phi=A\,dy\wedge dz+B\,dz\wedge dx+C\,dx\wedge dy.

[295-295 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
Pulling it back to the (u,v)-plane gives something times

[297-297 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
du\wedge dv.

[299-299 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
Start with

[301-301 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
dx=x_u\,du+x_v\,dv,

[303-303 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
dy=y_u\,du+y_v\,dv,

[305-305 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
and

[307-307 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
dz=z_u\,du+z_v\,dv.

[309-309 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
Then

[311-311 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\boxed{\mathbf r^*(dy\wedge dz) = (y_u z_v-y_v z_u)\,du\wedge dv,}

[313-313 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\boxed{\mathbf r^*(dz\wedge dx) = (z_u x_v-z_v x_u)\,du\wedge dv,}

[315-315 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
and

[317-317 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\boxed{\mathbf r^*(dx\wedge dy) = (x_u y_v-x_v y_u)\,du\wedge dv.}

[319-319 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
So

[321-321 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\boxed{\begin{aligned} \mathbf r^*\Phi \amp= \Bigl[ A(\mathbf r)(y_u z_v-y_v z_u) + B(\mathbf r)(z_u x_v-z_v x_u)\\ \amp\qquad\qquad + C(\mathbf r)(x_u y_v-x_v y_u) \Bigr]\,du\wedge dv. \end{aligned}}

[323-323 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
The three coefficients are the three components of the cross product

[325-325 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v.

[327-327 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
Thus, if

[329-329 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\Phi_{\mathbf F} = P\,dy\wedge dz+Q\,dz\wedge dx+R\,dx\wedge dy

[331-331 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
is the flux form of

[333-333 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\mathbf F=(P,Q,R),

[335-335 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
then

[337-337 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
\boxed{\mathbf r^*\Phi_{\mathbf F} = \mathbf F(\mathbf r(u,v))\cdot (\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v)\,du\wedge dv.}

[339-339 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
This is the classical flux formula, written as a pullback.

[341-341 title checkpoint-16-5-wedge-becomes-cross default_required]
Where did the cross product come from?

[343-349 p checkpoint-16-5-wedge-becomes-cross default_required]
Nothing in the setup of \mathbf r^*\Phi mentioned a cross product, yet the
          three coefficients turned out to be exactly the components of
          \mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v. What is it about pulling back a
          2-form through the wedge product that forces this cross product to appear on
          its own?

[352-356 p checkpoint-16-5-wedge-becomes-cross hint]
Each basic 2-form like dy\wedge dz pulls back to a
          2\times 2 determinant in the parameter derivatives. Recall how a cross
          product is assembled out of 2\times 2 determinants of the same columns.

[361-361 title c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
A tilted screen

[363-363 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
Let the surface be the tilted plane patch

[364-364 md c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
\mathbf r(u,v)=(u,v,u+2v),

[365-365 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
and let

[366-366 md c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
\Phi=dy\wedge dz+z\,dx\wedge dy.

[367-367 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
Compute the pullback.

[368-368 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
Here

[369-369 md c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
x=u, \qquad y=v, \qquad z=u+2v.

[370-370 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
Therefore

[371-371 md c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
dx=du, \qquad dy=dv, \qquad dz=du+2\,dv.

[372-372 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
Now

[373-377 md c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
\mathbf r^*(dy\wedge dz) \amp= dv\wedge(du+2\,dv)
          \amp= dv\wedge du+2\,dv\wedge dv
          \amp= -du\wedge dv.

[378-378 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
Also,

[379-379 md c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
\mathbf r^*(z\,dx\wedge dy) = (u+2v)\,du\wedge dv.

[380-380 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
So

[381-381 md c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
\boxed{\mathbf r^*\Phi=(u+2v-1)\,du\wedge dv.}

[382-385 p c16s5-ex-tilted-screen default_required]
The original form measured oriented surface pieces in space. The pulled-back form
          measures the corresponding parameter pieces in the (u,v)-plane.

[389-393 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-2forms-surfaces default_required]
The pullback is not just a computational convenience. It is what makes it possible to
      integrate a form over a curved surface by integrating an ordinary form over a flat
      parameter region.

[397-397 title c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
Pulling back volume forms under coordinate changes

[399-399 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
For a coordinate change in space,

[401-401 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
T(u,v,w)=(x(u,v,w),y(u,v,w),z(u,v,w)),

[403-403 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
the basic volume form pulls back to

[405-405 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
T^*(dx\wedge dy\wedge dz).

[407-407 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
Using

[409-409 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
dx=x_u\,du+x_v\,dv+x_w\,dw,

[411-411 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
and similarly for dy and dz, the wedge product gives

[413-413 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
\boxed{T^*(dx\wedge dy\wedge dz) = \frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)} \,du\wedge dv\wedge dw.}

[415-415 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
The coefficient is the Jacobian determinant:

[417-417 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
\boxed{\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)} = \det \begin{pmatrix} x_u \amp x_v \amp x_w\\ y_u \amp y_v \amp y_w\\ z_u \amp z_v \amp z_w \end{pmatrix}.}

[419-419 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
For a general 3-form

[421-421 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
\Omega=H(x,y,z)\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz,

[423-423 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
we get

[425-425 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
\boxed{T^*\Omega = H(T(u,v,w)) \frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)} \,du\wedge dv\wedge dw.}

[428-428 title c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
Cylindrical coordinates as a pullback

[430-430 p c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
In cylindrical coordinates,

[431-431 md c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
x=r\cos\theta, \qquad y=r\sin\theta, \qquad z=z.

[432-432 p c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
Then

[433-433 md c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
dx=\cos\theta\,dr-r\sin\theta\,d\theta,

[434-434 md c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
dy=\sin\theta\,dr+r\cos\theta\,d\theta,

[435-435 p c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
and

[436-436 md c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
dz=dz.

[437-437 p c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
Compute

[438-441 md c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
dx\wedge dy \amp= (\cos\theta\,dr-r\sin\theta\,d\theta) \wedge (\sin\theta\,dr+r\cos\theta\,d\theta)
          \amp= r\cos^2\theta\,dr\wedge d\theta - r\sin\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge dr.

[442-442 p c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
Since

[443-443 md c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
d\theta\wedge dr=-dr\wedge d\theta,

[444-444 p c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
we get

[445-448 md c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
dx\wedge dy \amp= r(\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta)\,dr\wedge d\theta
          \amp= r\,dr\wedge d\theta.

[449-449 p c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
Therefore

[450-450 md c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
\boxed{dx\wedge dy\wedge dz = r\,dr\wedge d\theta\wedge dz.}

[451-455 p c16s5-ex-cylindrical-coordinates-pullback default_required]
This is the cylindrical volume factor from
          , but now the wedge product has also kept the
          orientation.

[459-459 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
For ordinary volume, we write

[461-461 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
dV=r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz.

[463-463 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
For oriented volume, we write

[465-465 md c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
dx\wedge dy\wedge dz = r\,dr\wedge d\theta\wedge dz.

[467-467 p c16s5-subsec-pullback-volume-forms default_required]
The factor is the same. The wedge notation remembers the sign.

[471-471 title c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
The Jacobian determinant from wedge products

[473-476 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
The Jacobian determinant is not a mysterious correction factor. It is the coefficient
      of a pulled-back volume form.

[478-478 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
In two variables, let

[480-480 md c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
T(u,v)=(x(u,v),y(u,v)).

[482-482 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
Then

[484-484 md c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
dx=x_u\,du+x_v\,dv,

[486-486 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
and

[488-488 md c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
dy=y_u\,du+y_v\,dv.

[490-490 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
So

[492-496 md c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
T^*(dx\wedge dy) \amp= (x_u\,du+x_v\,dv)\wedge(y_u\,du+y_v\,dv)
      \amp= x_u y_v\,du\wedge dv+x_v y_u\,dv\wedge du
      \amp= (x_u y_v-x_v y_u)\,du\wedge dv.

[498-498 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
Thus

[500-500 md c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
\boxed{T^*(dx\wedge dy) = \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} \,du\wedge dv.}

[502-502 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
In three variables,

[504-504 md c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
\boxed{T^*(dx\wedge dy\wedge dz) = \frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)} \,du\wedge dv\wedge dw.}

[506-509 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
The absolute value of the Jacobian appears in ordinary area and volume computations
      because ordinary area and volume are unsigned:

[511-511 md c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
dA=\left|\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}\right|\,du\,dv,

[513-513 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
and

[515-515 md c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
dV=\left|\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)}\right|\,du\,dv\,dw.

[517-517 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
Forms do not automatically take absolute values. They measure orientation.

[519-519 title checkpoint-16-5-jacobian-sign default_required]
What does the Jacobian's sign mean?

[521-526 p checkpoint-16-5-jacobian-sign default_required]
When T^*(dx\wedge dy) produces a negative Jacobian coefficient, the
          ordinary change-of-variables formula would simply take its absolute value. What
          information is the bare wedge product holding onto that the absolute value throws
          away, and why might we sometimes want to keep it?

[529-533 p checkpoint-16-5-jacobian-sign hint]
Ask what a sign could possibly be recording about the map T itself. Picture a
          small loop traced counterclockwise in the (u,v)-square and what happens to its
          turning direction after T sends it into the (x,y)-plane.

[538-538 title c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
A reflection remembers its sign

[540-540 p c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
Let

[541-541 md c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
T(u,v)=(x,y)=(u,1-v).

[542-542 p c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
Then

[543-543 md c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
dx=du, \qquad dy=-dv.

[544-544 p c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
So

[545-545 md c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
\boxed{T^*(dx\wedge dy)=-du\wedge dv.}

[546-549 p c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
The map reflects the parameter square across a horizontal line. It preserves
          ordinary area but reverses orientation.

[550-553 p c16s5-ex-reflection-remembers-sign default_required]
If we were computing unsigned area, the scale factor would be 1. But for an
          oriented 2-form, the scale factor is -1.

[557-561 p c16s5-subsec-jacobian-from-wedge default_required]
A pullback therefore does two jobs at once. It performs substitution, and it keeps
      track of how oriented length, area, or volume changes. Once a form has been pulled back
      to a parameter domain, the only remaining step is to integrate it.
