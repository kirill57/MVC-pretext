[5-5 title sec-appA-the-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus default_required]
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

[8-8 title appA-ftc-subsec-definite-integral default_required]
Definite integral

[10-14 p appA-ftc-subsec-definite-integral default_required]
The definite integral
      \boxed{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx}
      is the signed accumulation of f from a to b.

[16-18 p appA-ftc-subsec-definite-integral default_required]
If f(x)\ge0, it is area under the graph.

[20-24 p appA-ftc-subsec-definite-integral default_required]
If f is a rate, then
      \int_a^b f(x)\,dx
      is total change.

[28-28 title appA-ftc-subsec-basic-properties default_required]
Basic properties

[30-32 p appA-ftc-subsec-basic-properties default_required]
\int_a^b (f+g)\,dx = \int_a^b f\,dx+\int_a^b g\,dx

[34-36 p appA-ftc-subsec-basic-properties default_required]
\int_a^b cf\,dx = c\int_a^b f\,dx

[38-40 p appA-ftc-subsec-basic-properties default_required]
\int_a^b f\,dx = -\int_b^a f\,dx

[42-44 p appA-ftc-subsec-basic-properties default_required]
\int_a^a f\,dx=0

[46-48 p appA-ftc-subsec-basic-properties default_required]
\int_a^b f\,dx = \int_a^c f\,dx+\int_c^b f\,dx

[50-55 p appA-ftc-subsec-basic-properties default_required]
If
      f(x)\ge0
      on [a,b], then
      \int_a^b f(x)\,dx\ge0.

[57-62 p appA-ftc-subsec-basic-properties default_required]
If
      f(x)\le g(x)
      on [a,b], then
      \int_a^b f(x)\,dx\le \int_a^b g(x)\,dx.

[66-66 title appA-ftc-subsec-ftc-part-i default_required]
Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part I

[68-73 p appA-ftc-subsec-ftc-part-i default_required]
If f is continuous and
      A(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt,
      then
      \boxed{A'(x)=f(x).}

[75-77 p appA-ftc-subsec-ftc-part-i default_required]
Accumulation differentiates back to the integrand.

[81-81 title appA-ftc-subsec-ftc-part-ii default_required]
Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part II

[83-89 p appA-ftc-subsec-ftc-part-ii default_required]
If
      F'(x)=f(x),
      with f continuous on [a,b] and F continuous at the endpoints,
      then
      \boxed{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a).}

[91-94 p appA-ftc-subsec-ftc-part-ii default_required]
Equivalently,
      \boxed{\int_a^b F'(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a).}

[96-98 p appA-ftc-subsec-ftc-part-ii default_required]
This is the net change theorem.

[102-102 title appA-ftc-subsec-boundary-form default_required]
Boundary form

[104-107 p appA-ftc-subsec-boundary-form default_required]
The oriented boundary of an interval is
      \boxed{\partial[a,b]=\{b\}-\{a\}.}

[109-112 p appA-ftc-subsec-boundary-form default_required]
So the Fundamental Theorem can be read as
      \boxed{\int_{[a,b]} dF=\int_{\partial[a,b]}F.}

[114-117 p appA-ftc-subsec-boundary-form default_required]
This is the one-dimensional model for the generalized Stokes theorem
      ().

[121-121 title appA-ftc-subsec-average-value default_required]
Average value

[123-126 p appA-ftc-subsec-average-value default_required]
The average value of f on [a,b] is
      \boxed{f_{\operatorname{avg}} = \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.}

[130-130 title appA-ftc-subsec-common-antiderivatives default_required]
Common antiderivatives

[132-134 p appA-ftc-subsec-common-antiderivatives default_required]
\int x^n\,dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C \qquad (n\ne -1)

[136-138 p appA-ftc-subsec-common-antiderivatives default_required]
\int \frac1x\,dx=\ln|x|+C

[140-142 p appA-ftc-subsec-common-antiderivatives default_required]
\int e^x\,dx=e^x+C

[144-146 p appA-ftc-subsec-common-antiderivatives default_required]
\int \sin x\,dx=-\cos x+C

[148-150 p appA-ftc-subsec-common-antiderivatives default_required]
\int \cos x\,dx=\sin x+C

[152-154 p appA-ftc-subsec-common-antiderivatives default_required]
\int \sec^2x\,dx=\tan x+C

[158-158 title appA-ftc-subsec-warnings default_required]
Warnings

[160-162 p appA-ftc-subsec-warnings default_required]
Do not forget the constant C in an indefinite integral.

[164-166 p appA-ftc-subsec-warnings default_required]
Do not put +C on a definite integral.

[168-172 p appA-ftc-subsec-warnings default_required]
The definite integral
      \int_a^b f(x)\,dx
      depends on the orientation of the interval. Reversing the endpoints changes the sign.
