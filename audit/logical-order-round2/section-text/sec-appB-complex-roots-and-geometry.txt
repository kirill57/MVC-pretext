[5-5 title sec-appB-complex-roots-and-geometry default_required]
Complex roots and geometry

[8-8 title subsec-appB-roots-nth-roots-of-a-complex-number default_required]
nth roots of a complex number

[10-17 p subsec-appB-roots-nth-roots-of-a-complex-number default_required]
Let
      w=Re^{i\Theta}, \qquad R>0.
      The solutions of
      z^n=w
      are
      \boxed{ z_k = R^{1/n}e^{i(\Theta+2\pi k)/n}, \qquad k=0,1,\ldots,n-1. }

[19-19 p subsec-appB-roots-nth-roots-of-a-complex-number default_required]
There are n distinct roots.

[21-24 p subsec-appB-roots-nth-roots-of-a-complex-number default_required]
They lie on the circle of radius
      R^{1/n}.

[26-29 p subsec-appB-roots-nth-roots-of-a-complex-number default_required]
Their angles are spaced by
      \boxed{ \frac{2\pi}{n}. }

[33-33 title subsec-appB-roots-roots-of-unity default_required]
Roots of unity

[35-40 p subsec-appB-roots-roots-of-unity default_required]
The solutions of
      z^n=1
      are
      \boxed{ z_k=e^{2\pi i k/n}, \qquad k=0,1,\ldots,n-1. }

[42-42 p subsec-appB-roots-roots-of-unity default_required]
These are the nth roots of unity.

[44-44 p subsec-appB-roots-roots-of-unity default_required]
They form a regular n-gon on the unit circle.

[48-48 title subsec-appB-roots-primitive-root default_required]
Primitive root

[50-56 p subsec-appB-roots-primitive-root default_required]
\boxed{ \omega=e^{2\pi i/n} }
      Then the nth roots of unity are
      \boxed{ 1,\omega,\omega^2,\ldots,\omega^{n-1}. }
      Also,
      \boxed{ \omega^n=1. }

[60-60 title subsec-appB-roots-sum-of-roots-of-unity default_required]
Sum of roots of unity

[62-69 p subsec-appB-roots-sum-of-roots-of-unity default_required]
For n>1,
      \boxed{ 1+\omega+\omega^2+\cdots+\omega^{n-1}=0. }
      Reason:
      1+\omega+\cdots+\omega^{n-1} = \frac{1-\omega^n}{1-\omega} = 0,
      because
      \omega^n=1 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \omega\ne1.

[73-73 title subsec-appB-roots-square-roots default_required]
Square roots

[75-80 p subsec-appB-roots-square-roots default_required]
To solve
      z^2=Re^{i\Theta},
      use
      \boxed{ z_0=\sqrt R e^{i\Theta/2}, \qquad z_1=\sqrt R e^{i(\Theta+2\pi)/2}. }

[82-82 p subsec-appB-roots-square-roots default_required]
The two roots are opposites.

[86-86 title subsec-appB-roots-examples default_required]
Examples

[88-91 p subsec-appB-roots-examples default_required]
Fourth roots of unity:
      \boxed{ 1,\quad i,\quad -1,\quad -i. }

[93-96 p subsec-appB-roots-examples default_required]
Cube roots of unity:
      \boxed{ 1,\quad -\frac12+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}i,\quad -\frac12-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}i. }

[98-107 p subsec-appB-roots-examples default_required]
Solutions of
      z^3=8:
      write
      8=8e^{i0}.
      Then
      z_k=2e^{2\pi i k/3}, \qquad k=0,1,2.
      So
      \boxed{ z=2,\quad -1+\sqrt3\,i,\quad -1-\sqrt3\,i. }

[109-118 p subsec-appB-roots-examples default_required]
Solutions of
      z^3=-8:
      write
      -8=8e^{i\pi}.
      Then
      z_k=2e^{i(\pi+2\pi k)/3}, \qquad k=0,1,2.
      So
      \boxed{ z=1+\sqrt3\,i,\quad -2,\quad 1-\sqrt3\,i. }

[122-122 title subsec-appB-roots-geometric-meaning default_required]
Geometric meaning

[124-130 p subsec-appB-roots-geometric-meaning default_required]
The map
      z\mapsto z^n
      does two things:
      \boxed{ \text{length } r \mapsto r^n }
      \boxed{ \text{angle } \theta \mapsto n\theta. }

[132-136 p subsec-appB-roots-geometric-meaning default_required]
Taking nth roots reverses those operations:
      \boxed{ \text{length } R \mapsto R^{1/n} }
      \boxed{ \text{angle } \Theta \mapsto \frac{\Theta+2\pi k}{n}. }

[138-138 p subsec-appB-roots-geometric-meaning default_required]
The extra 2\pi k is why there are n roots.
