[5-5 title sec-appF-fubini-s-theorem-and-change-of-variables default_required]
Fubini's theorem and change of variables

[8-8 title appF-fubini-fubinis-theorem-statement default_required]
Fubini's theorem: statement

[10-23 p appF-fubini-fubinis-theorem-statement default_required]
If f is continuous on a rectangle
      R=[a,b]\times[c,d],
      then
      
        \boxed{
          \iint_R f(x,y)\,dA
          =
          \int_a^b\int_c^d f(x,y)\,dy\,dx
          =
          \int_c^d\int_a^b f(x,y)\,dx\,dy.
        }

[25-34 p appF-fubini-fubinis-theorem-statement default_required]
For a solid box,
      
        \boxed{
          \iiint_B f\,dV
        }
      
      can be computed by any order of iterated integration, as long as the corresponding
      bounds describe the same box.

[38-38 title appF-fubini-fubini-hypotheses default_required]
Fubini: hypotheses

[40-40 p appF-fubini-fubini-hypotheses default_required]
For the version used most often in this book:

[42-45 md appF-fubini-fubini-hypotheses default_required]
1. \amp \text{The region is a rectangle, box, or a region described by clean bounds.}
      2. \amp \text{The function is continuous on the compact region.}

[49-49 title appF-fubini-fubini-proof-idea default_required]
Fubini: proof idea

[51-54 p appF-fubini-fubini-proof-idea default_required]
A double integral is a limit of sums over small rectangles:
      \sum f(x_i^*,y_j^*)\,\Delta x\,\Delta y.

[56-65 p appF-fubini-fubini-proof-idea default_required]
Group the same sum by columns:
      
        \sum_i
        \left(
          \sum_j f(x_i^*,y_j^*)\,\Delta y
        \right)
        \Delta x.

[67-70 p appF-fubini-fubini-proof-idea default_required]
The inner sum becomes an integral in y. The outer sum becomes an integral in
      x.

[72-72 p appF-fubini-fubini-proof-idea default_required]
Group by rows instead, and the order reverses.

[76-76 title appF-fubini-fubini-dependencies default_required]
Fubini: dependencies

[78-82 md appF-fubini-fubini-dependencies default_required]
1. \amp \text{Riemann sums.}
      2. \amp \text{Single-variable integration.}
      3. \amp \text{Continuity or integrability.}

[86-86 title appF-fubini-fubini-what-can-fail default_required]
Fubini: what can fail

[88-88 p appF-fubini-fubini-what-can-fail default_required]
For continuous functions on compact regions with piecewise smooth boundary, Fubini is safe.

[90-93 p appF-fubini-fubini-what-can-fail default_required]
For improper integrals, changing the order can change the value if the integral is not
      absolutely convergent.

[95-95 p appF-fubini-fubini-what-can-fail default_required]
The safe rule is:

[97-101 md appF-fubini-fubini-what-can-fail default_required]
\boxed{
        \text{If }\iint |f|\,dA\text{ is finite, changing order is safe.}
      }

[103-103 p appF-fubini-fubini-what-can-fail default_required]
Without absolute convergence, be careful.

[107-107 title appF-fubini-change-of-variables-statement default_required]
Change of variables: statement

[109-125 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-statement default_required]
For a C^1 coordinate map
      T(u,v)=(x(u,v),y(u,v)),
      the change of variables formula is
      
        \boxed{
          \iint_R f(x,y)\,dA
          =
          \iint_{\widetilde R}
          f(T(u,v))
          \left|
            \det DT(u,v)
          \right|
          \,du\,dv.
        }

[127-136 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-statement default_required]
In differential forms language:
      
        \boxed{
          T^*(dx\wedge dy)
          =
          \det DT\,du\wedge dv.
        }

[138-148 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-statement default_required]
When the orientation on R is transported from \widetilde R by T, the oriented formula is
      
        \boxed{
          \iint_R f\,dx\wedge dy
          =
          \iint_{\widetilde R}
          f(T(u,v))\det DT\,du\wedge dv.
        }

[150-153 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-statement default_required]
The ordinary area formula uses
      |\det DT|.

[157-157 title appF-fubini-change-of-variables-hypotheses default_required]
Change of variables: hypotheses

[159-159 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-hypotheses default_required]
The standard version assumes:

[161-166 md appF-fubini-change-of-variables-hypotheses default_required]
1. \amp T\text{ is }C^1.
      2. \amp T\text{ is one-to-one on the region, except possibly on boundary pieces.}
      3. \amp \det DT\ne0\text{ in the interior.}
      4. \amp \text{The regions are compact with piecewise smooth boundary; }T(\widetilde R)=R.

[170-170 title appF-fubini-change-of-variables-proof-idea default_required]
Change of variables: proof idea

[172-183 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-proof-idea default_required]
Near a point (u,v), the map T is almost linear:
      
        T(u+\Delta u,v+\Delta v)
        \approx
        T(u,v)+DT
        \begin{pmatrix}
        \Delta u\\
        \Delta v
        \end{pmatrix}.

[185-188 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-proof-idea default_required]
A tiny rectangle in the uv-plane becomes almost a parallelogram in the
      xy-plane.

[190-197 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-proof-idea default_required]
The area scale factor of that parallelogram is
      
        \boxed{
          |\det DT|.
        }

[199-199 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-proof-idea default_required]
Add over many tiny rectangles and pass to the limit.

[203-203 title appF-fubini-change-of-variables-dependencies default_required]
Change of variables: dependencies

[205-210 md appF-fubini-change-of-variables-dependencies default_required]
1. \amp \text{Differentiability as local linear approximation.}
      2. \amp \text{Determinant as area or volume scale factor.}
      3. \amp \text{Riemann sums.}
      4. \amp \text{One-to-one coordinate descriptions.}

[214-214 title appF-fubini-change-of-variables-what-can-fail default_required]
Change of variables: what can fail

[216-216 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-what-can-fail default_required]
If the map is not one-to-one, the integral may count points more than once.

[218-222 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-what-can-fail default_required]
Example: polar coordinates with
      0\le \theta\le4\pi
      trace a full disk twice.

[224-224 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-what-can-fail default_required]
If the Jacobian vanishes on a whole region, the map may collapse area.

[226-229 p appF-fubini-change-of-variables-what-can-fail default_required]
If the orientation matters, do not replace \det DT by |\det DT|. The sign is
      part of the form.

[233-233 title appF-fubini-moral default_required]
Moral

[235-238 p appF-fubini-moral default_required]
Fubini explains how to add by slices. Change of variables explains how small pieces
      change size and orientation under a new coordinate language.
